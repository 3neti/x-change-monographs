# Marketing Publication Maturity Roadmap

## Purpose

This roadmap recommends the order in which the publishing system should mature before and around the creation of marketing collateral. It does not create collateral, prescribe a directory scaffold, or choose implementation tools.

The sequence is designed to preserve the repository's accepted architecture:

```text
Evidence and Source Recovery
        ↓
Editorial Authorities
        ↓
Claims and Approval Authority
        ↓
Messaging Architecture
        ↓
Audience Grammar and Vocabulary
        ↓
Canonical Stories and Proof
        ↓
Campaign and Collateral Governance
        ↓
Publication Profiles and Visual Language
        ↓
First Brochure Pilot
        ↓
Institutional Briefs and Teasers
        ↓
Publication Automation and Release Catalogue
```

## Guiding maturity rule

Each stage should make the next stage safer and less interpretive. A stage is mature when its authority, owner, evidence, and approval state are clear enough that downstream work can derive from it without inventing upstream meaning.

## Stage 0 — Evidence and source recovery

### Objective

Establish which existing x-change writings and publication artifacts are authoritative and bring the repository's declared source locations into correspondence with reality.

### Work to complete

- Identify the approved content referenced by the editorial-canon and manuscript-specification placeholders.
- Locate and authenticate the Volume I author manuscript, editorial composition, and externally typeset material described by its metadata.
- Locate and authenticate the approved Volume II author manuscript and any editorial movement content.
- Determine whether significant founder writings, execution documents, financial-planning materials, investor materials, or other source collections exist outside this repository.
- Record provenance and authority before treating an external artifact as a canonical source.

### Maturity gate

The institution can state which texts are authoritative, which remain drafts, who approved them, and where the preserved originals reside. Placeholder titles are not being used as substitutes for a corpus.

### Why this comes first

Editorial and marketing authorities must be informed by actual institutional writing. Recovery also prevents new materials from unintentionally superseding work that already exists elsewhere.

## Stage 1 — Editorial authorities

### Objective

Make the editorial canon and editorial manuscript specification operational authorities rather than placeholders.

### Work to complete

- Confirm the approved editorial principles and the authority responsible for them.
- Confirm the specification for a canonical editorial manuscript, including approval and exception handling.
- Reconcile both authorities with the three accepted decisions.
- Clarify the lifecycle relationship among author manuscript, editorial manuscript, publication master, released edition, and generated build.

### Maturity gate

An editor can determine whether a proposed source or editorial artifact conforms to the system without relying on unwritten custom.

### Why this follows source recovery

Authorities should govern a known body of work and preserve its successful distinctions. Completing them before marketing design keeps campaign needs from becoming the accidental editorial standard.

## Stage 2 — Claims and approval authority

### Objective

Establish how ideas become public claims and who is accountable for their approval.

### Work to complete

- Distinguish founder thesis, institutional position, current capability, aspiration, evidence, and regulated or legally sensitive statement.
- Define the evidence expected for reusable claims.
- Identify review responsibilities for editorial, operational, legal, financial, and founder-level assertions where applicable.
- Define how approvals expire, are superseded, or require renewed review.

### Maturity gate

Every proposed public assertion can be classified, sourced, reviewed by the appropriate authority, and either approved, qualified, or rejected.

### Why this precedes messaging

Messaging architecture amplifies and repeats ideas. Claims boundaries must be known before repetition turns an ambiguous thesis into an apparent institutional promise.

## Stage 3 — Messaging architecture

### Objective

Create one governed hierarchy connecting institutional meaning to usable public messages.

### Work to complete

- Establish the core institutional proposition and its relationship to the monograph theses.
- Define the problem, change, value, principles, reasons to believe, and invitation at appropriate levels of abstraction.
- Separate universal messages from audience-, product-, campaign-, and format-specific expressions.
- Link each substantive message to canonical source and approved evidence.

### Maturity gate

Two editors working independently can derive materially consistent messages, and each message can be traced to authority rather than personal preference.

### Why this follows claims authority

The message hierarchy can now be ambitious without losing the distinction between thesis, proof, and current organizational fact.

## Stage 4 — Audience grammar and vocabulary

### Objective

Define how one institutional meaning is responsibly expressed to different readers.

### Work to complete

- Identify priority audiences and the situations in which they encounter x-change.
- Describe their likely knowledge, concerns, responsibilities, questions, and appropriate next relationship.
- Define which message layers remain invariant and which can change in emphasis or depth.
- Establish preferred, conditional, deprecated, and prohibited terms, including rules for institutional and financial-settlement language.
- Clarify naming conventions for x-change, x-change OS, the monograph series, participants, businesses, institutions, and other relevant actors.

### Maturity gate

An editor can adapt a core message for a named audience without changing its meaning, overstating a claim, or improvising key terminology.

### Why audience and vocabulary mature together

Words take meaning in audience context. A vocabulary authority without audience grammar becomes rigid; audience profiles without vocabulary authority fragment the voice.

## Stage 5 — Canonical stories and proof

### Objective

Give the message architecture concrete, reusable narrative and evidentiary material.

### Work to complete

- Select founder observations, ordinary situations, institutional problems, examples, and outcomes from authoritative sources.
- Record the source, factual status, permissions, approved framing, and audience relevance of each story.
- Associate proof with the claims it supports and state its limitations.
- Distinguish illustrative scenarios from real people, customers, partners, or results.

### Maturity gate

A writer can choose an approved story or proof element, understand exactly what it supports, and preserve its attribution and limits across formats.

### Why this follows messaging and audiences

Story selection becomes purposeful only after the institution knows what must be explained and to whom. This stage turns abstract architecture into human material without letting anecdote dictate strategy.

## Stage 6 — Campaign and collateral governance

### Objective

Define how marketing publications relate to one another, to campaigns, and to their sources.

### Work to complete

- Define the purpose and relationship of brochure, institutional brief, teaser, presentation, web publication, and other approved collateral types.
- Establish ownership, audience, campaign, source lineage, status, approval, edition, release, and supersession concepts.
- Define how derivatives inherit or revalidate claims, stories, and vocabulary.
- Establish a collateral index and a way to identify the current approved edition.

### Maturity gate

Before a piece is commissioned, the team can explain why it exists, who it serves, what it derives from, who approves it, and how a later reader will know whether it is current.

### Why this precedes design and drafting

Governance prevents the first brochure's practical choices from silently becoming the architecture for every later format.

## Stage 7 — Publication profiles and visual language

### Objective

Translate editorial and collateral architecture into explicit rules for form.

### Work to complete

- Define the relationship between monograph identity and marketing-publication identity.
- Establish typography, hierarchy, layout, imagery, plates, captions, data presentation, color, accessibility, and attribution principles.
- Define output-specific profiles for the formats actually needed, including print and screen behavior.
- Establish asset provenance, rights, source quality, transformation, and accessibility requirements.
- Define publication-master and release-quality expectations without coupling them to one rendering tool prematurely.

### Maturity gate

The same approved editorial source can be composed into a named format predictably, and visual choices reinforce rather than redefine the message.

### Why this follows collateral governance

Form should answer a known communication purpose. Once collateral types and audiences are explicit, profiles can be precise rather than generic.

## Stage 8 — First brochure pilot

### Objective

Test the complete authority-to-release chain with one deliberately bounded marketing publication.

### Work to complete

- Select one priority audience and one clearly defined communication purpose.
- Derive the brief from approved messaging, vocabulary, stories, and claims.
- Produce distinct editorial source, publication master, and release candidate artifacts in accordance with the accepted separation decision.
- Run editorial, claim, visual, accessibility, and release review.
- Record exceptions and lessons as proposed changes to upstream authorities rather than silently embedding them in the brochure.

### Maturity gate

The brochure is approved, source-traceable, reproducible, clearly editioned, and usable by its intended audience. The team can name what the pilot proved and what must change upstream.

### Why the first brochure appears here

At this stage the brochure validates a publishing system instead of inventing one. Keeping the pilot bounded reveals which standards are real and which remain theoretical.

## Stage 9 — Institutional briefs and teasers

### Objective

Extend the proven system into a coherent family of shorter and more specialized publications.

### Work to complete

- Develop institutional briefs for audiences that need greater evidence, specificity, or formal context.
- Develop teasers only as governed derivatives that point toward a fuller source or next action.
- Test cross-format consistency, audience adaptation, source lineage, and supersession behavior.
- Expand the story and proof library only through the same authority process.

### Maturity gate

Multiple formats can express one institutional position at different depths without contradiction, unsupported compression, or visual fragmentation.

### Why briefs precede broad automation

The second and third publication types reveal the real variation in the system. Automation designed after this variation is more likely to encode stable common structure rather than one brochure's accidents.

## Stage 10 — Publication automation and release catalogue

### Objective

Make the proven process reproducible, inspectable, and discoverable at scale.

### Work to complete

- Validate metadata, source references, maturity, approvals, asset provenance, and absence of placeholder content.
- Generate approved outputs from publication masters where the process is deterministic.
- Perform format-appropriate quality checks for links, typography, images, accessibility, pagination, and file identity.
- Record editions, releases, checksums or equivalent identities, source revisions, and supersession.
- Expand status reporting from directory presence to publishing readiness and catalogue discovery.

### Maturity gate

An authorized release can be regenerated from canonical inputs, verified consistently, located in a catalogue, and traced back to the exact approved sources and decisions.

### Why automation comes last

The current repository correctly reserves output and script boundaries but has not yet defined the process they would encode. Automating after the editorial, marketing, visual, and release models have survived real publications avoids hardening placeholder assumptions.

## Recommended immediate next step

Begin **Stage 0: Evidence and source recovery** as a bounded editorial audit. Its output should be an authority decision about the existing canon, specification, Volume I materials, Volume II materials, and any external x-change source corpus. Do not draft collateral during that audit.

Once the source set is known, complete the two existing editorial-authority placeholders before introducing a marketing-specific authority. This preserves the repository's current architecture and gives later messaging work a legitimate foundation.
