## 1. Program Purpose

The Government Depository Digital Banking Program helps trusted government depository institutions administer public money more effectively through accountable, institution-controlled, digitally coordinated financial infrastructure.

Its purpose is not simply to digitize banking. Government entities already use digital channels, electronic transfers, account systems, payment providers, and reporting processes. The institutional question is whether an eligible depository institution can combine lawful authority, public trust, operating discipline, and maintained infrastructure into a reusable capability for public-finance workflows.

Public money carries purpose, authority, conditions, and an obligation to account for its result. A collection must be attributed correctly. A disbursement must proceed under valid authority. An exception must remain visible. A completed transaction must return evidence sufficient for the responsible government entity, depository institution, Treasury and finance functions, reconciliation, review, and audit.

The Program therefore exists to strengthen the capacity of government depository institutions to participate responsibly in public finance. Technology is an enabling layer. The product at program level is the institution's increased ability to carry public authority into accountable financial execution.

The Program does not confer eligibility, depository authority, selection, procurement status, settlement access, or public trust. Each remains subject to the applicable legal, regulatory, policy, institutional, commercial, and operating authority.

## 2. Program Vision

The intended end state is a field of capable and eligible government depository institutions able to support selected public-finance workflows through the same maintained x-change platform without becoming one shared institution.

A mature Program may support multiple institutions. Each institution would operate through its own authority, approved deployment, data, credentials, policies, personnel, production decisions, provider relationships, audit trail, and public obligations. Common software would reduce repeated engineering. It would not consolidate banking authority, public funds, production accounts, customer or agency relationships, or institutional accountability.

The Program should allow a government entity to evaluate a depository institution as a credible operating option for a particular workflow. The evaluation would consider authority, eligibility, financial standing, governance, institutional fit, controls, service design, infrastructure, security, settlement arrangements, reconciliation, continuity, price, evidence, and performance.

For the participating institution, maturity means being able to examine a new public-finance responsibility without rebuilding the entire operating foundation. It can begin with one bounded workflow, establish evidence, and expand only when the responsible authorities determine that the institution is ready.

For government, maturity means greater institutional choice within a common standard of accountability. Public-finance resilience improves when more than one qualified institution can support appropriate workflows without weakening public control.

## 3. Institutional Scope

The relevant field is broader than one ownership model or charter.

It may include:

- government-owned depository banks operating under their applicable mandates;
- private commercial banks possessing official government-depository authority under a charter or other applicable authority;
- banks with specialized charters, constituencies, or public-service purposes;
- Islamic banking institutions whose products, governance, and operations must comply with their distinct legal and Shari'ah foundations; and
- other banking institutions eligible to receive or administer government funds under applicable law, regulation, rule, exception, designation, or approved arrangement.

The Program does not treat these institutions as interchangeable. Ownership, charter, mandate, governance, customer base, public constituency, geographic reach, product model, balance sheet, risk appetite, technology capability, settlement arrangements, and institutional relationships remain consequential.

Eligibility is also distinct from selection. An institution may possess authority to participate in a class of government-depository activity without being qualified, selected, contracted, or operationally ready for a particular workflow. The Program preserves that distinction throughout.

Government entities likewise differ. A national agency, Local Government Unit, Government-Owned or Controlled Corporation, social-insurance institution, public educational body, government hospital, Treasury office, or other public institution may act under different authorities, fund structures, procurement rules, reporting requirements, recipient populations, and accountability mechanisms.

The Program supplies a common institutional framework within which those differences can be examined. It does not erase them through a generic technology proposition.

## 4. Target Institutions

The broad audience includes institutions that are or may be eligible to perform government-depository or related public-finance responsibilities.

The future field of institution-specific assessment may include:

## 5. Program Emotional Center

The emotional center of the Program is public trust.

> Public money deserves public trust.
>
> Trust requires accountability.
>
> Accountability requires evidence.
>
> Evidence requires modern infrastructure.

This sequence is not a slogan added to a technology program. It is the institutional logic that determines whether the technology has value.

Public trust begins with lawful authority and responsible officials. It continues through controls, segregation of responsibilities, traceable approvals, accurate execution, visible exceptions, reconciliation, and records that survive changes in personnel and leadership. It is sustained through performance over time.

Infrastructure contributes when it makes authority, financial state, conditions, execution, and evidence easier to govern. It fails when it makes responsibility less legible, encourages unsupported automation, conceals unresolved outcomes, or treats public accountability as a reporting feature added after settlement.

The Program must therefore remain institution-first. The government entity owns its public purpose. The depository institution owns its banking responsibilities. Technology providers support those responsibilities without acquiring them.

## 6. Program Product

The Program's product is institutional capability.

That capability is the maintained ability of an eligible government depository institution to receive or recognize a properly authorized public-finance instruction, apply institutional policy and conditions, coordinate execution through approved regulated arrangements, preserve status and evidence, manage exceptions, support reconciliation, and remain accountable for the part of the workflow within its authority.

x-change, APIs, Pay Codes, execution drivers, Settlement Envelopes, Treasury state, voucher-mediated journeys, reconciliation records, and evidence are components or expressions of that capability. None is the program-level product by itself.

A **Pay Code** remains a credential representing an authorized instruction. It is not money, a public fund, a deposit, a voucher balance, or a government benefit.

A public-facing voucher or credential journey does not create a new voucher execution architecture. The governing meaning remains in the Facility; conditions and evidence remain in the Settlement Envelope; operational financial state remains in Treasury; and Execution proceeds through the applicable approved engine, driver, and settlement relationship.

The institutional capability also includes people, policy, governance, security, operations, support, accounting, reconciliation, continuity, provider management, and public accountability. Software can coordinate the ceremony. It cannot replace the institution's obligation to govern it.

## 7. Strategic Outcomes

The Program is intended to make several outcomes more attainable without presenting any of them as guaranteed.

### Stronger government relationships

An institution able to define, execute, evidence, and reconcile a public workflow may present a stronger operating proposition to government entities it is authorized to serve. The relationship can extend beyond account maintenance into a governed service lifecycle.

### Eligibility for more complex workflows

Modern infrastructure may remove operating limitations that make a legally eligible institution difficult to consider for a complex program. It does not create legal eligibility or replace qualification.

### Preferred-institutional-option positioning

A specialized or trusted institution may seek to become a preferred institutional option in selected areas where its authority, identity, geographic relevance, constituency, service design, controls, and operating capability present a strong fit. Preference must still be earned through lawful process, qualification, selection, price, agreement, service, and performance.

### Deposits and recurring activity

Some public programs require accounts, balances, funding arrangements, collection flows, or recurring disbursements. Successful institutional relationships may therefore contribute deposits and transaction activity. Those are consequences of actual program structures and use, not guaranteed outputs of software licensing.

### Reconciliation and evidentiary strength

A common operating record can improve the institution's ability to relate authority, instruction, provider response, status, exception, and final reconciliation. The evidence must still satisfy the responsible institution, government entity, auditor, and applicable requirements.

### Reach beyond branch limitations

Institution-controlled digital workflows may allow a depository institution to support dispersed agencies, communities, and beneficiaries through approved channels and providers. Reach remains subject to channel availability, inclusion, service design, continuity, and the practical circumstances of participants.

### Public-finance resilience and choice

A broader field of capable institutions may reduce dependence on a narrow set of arrangements and give government entities more credible options. Diversity is valuable only when standards remain strong.

Every strategic outcome remains conditional upon authority, institutional eligibility, qualification, selection, procurement or approved engagement, agreement, implementation, operational readiness, adoption, and sustained performance.

## 8. Program Architecture

The Program applies the canonical x-change architecture without a sector-specific fork.

| Canonical concern | Government Depository application | Boundary preserved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instruction model | Represents an authorized financial instruction within a defined public-finance Facility | Does not create public authority or appropriations |
| Funding Event | Records the external event through which Settlement Resources enter or become recognized by Treasury | Does not manufacture funds or determine their legal character |
| Treasury | Maintains the operational state of recognized, available, reserved, outstanding, unresolved, and resolved resources | Is not the government account, bank account, or general ledger |
| Lien | Reserves operational capacity for an authorized purpose | Does not transfer custody |
| Facility | Carries public-finance purpose, participants, policy, limits, service responsibility, and approved commercial meaning | Does not replace the governing program or legal authority |
| Settlement Envelope | Carries approvals, identity, documents, limits, signatures, recipient basis, and other evidence or conditions | Does not perform settlement |
| Pay Code | Represents an authorized instruction as a credential | Is not money, a benefit, a deposit, or stored value |
| Voucher-mediated journey | Presents or communicates an approved instruction or entitlement within the governing Facility and Envelope | Does not create a separate voucher balance or execution model |
| Execution Engine | Coordinates the supported progression toward the approved outcome | Does not become the settlement institution |
| Execution Driver | Maps the common execution contract to an institution's approved API and provider behavior | Does not provide authority or operational readiness |
| Settlement model | Uses the institution's approved regulated settlement accounts, providers, rails, limits, credentials, and authority | Does not create an x-change settlement account |
| Evidence and reconciliation | Preserves operating and provider evidence for support, exception resolution, reconciliation, reporting, and audit | Does not replace institutional accounting or audit judgment |
| Institution onboarding | Establishes institution-specific discovery, deployment, integration, controls, roles, and readiness | Does not presume adoption or standardize away institutional differences |
| Submission architecture | Packages approved institution-specific publications through the common manifest-driven builder | Does not create new editions of frozen source publications |

The canonical operating progression remains:

```text
External financial event
        ↓
Funding Event
        ↓
Settlement Resources recognized by Treasury
        ↓
Lien reserves resources for an authorized purpose
        ↓
Facility defines the public-finance service
        ↓
Settlement Envelope establishes evidence and conditions
        ↓
Pay Code or other approved presentation carries the instruction
        ↓
Execution coordinates the authorized outcome
        ↓
Evidence, exception resolution, and reconciliation
```

Public-finance context changes the Facility, participants, authority, evidence, service obligations, and controls. It does not redefine the grammar.

## 9. Operating Model

The operating doctrine is:

> Technology can be shared without the institution becoming shared.
>
> Software may be common. Operations remain sovereign.

The default operating model combines maintained licensed software with institution-controlled operation.

### Licensed software

ODTI licenses and maintains x-change, the canonical operating grammar, supported components, documentation, reference architecture, testing capability, and agreed integrations or institution-specific drivers. Licensing does not convey banking authority, government-depository authority, settlement access, custody, production approval, or public mandate.

### Institution-controlled deployment

Each institution owns or controls its approved cloud or hosting account, domains, databases, data, credentials, encryption and access configuration, monitoring, logs, backups, recovery, billing relationship, provider accounts, audit trail, and production release authority according to its architecture and policy.

Separate deployments remain the default. A common codebase does not require common application tenancy, databases, production credentials, customer records, government-program records, or audit trails.

### Institution-owned CI/CD and authorization

The institution controls the route by which a maintained release is reviewed, tested, staged, authorized, deployed, monitored, and, when necessary, rolled back. ODTI may publish releases and support validation. The bank decides whether and when a release enters production.

### Institution-specific integration

The common execution contract requires an institution-specific driver, conceptually represented as `3neti/emi-{institution}`. The driver is not automatically available. It requires discovery, mapping, implementation, certification, maintenance, and controlled version evolution against the institution's approved interfaces and operating requirements.

### Regulated settlement providers

Settlement providers remain distinct participants responsible for the accounts, rails, services, records, and regulatory obligations within their authority. The depository institution owns or establishes the applicable relationship, credentials, limits, and execution authority. A shared regulated rail does not create shared software tenancy or shared institutional operation.

### Managed DevOps

A DevOps or managed-service provider may perform delegated provisioning, deployment, observability, backup, recovery, release support, and incident work. Its authority must be explicit, limited, logged, reviewable, and revocable. Delegation does not transfer ownership of the environment, data, secrets, release decision, or institutional accountability.

### Public-finance responsibility

The government entity retains authority for the public program, purpose, funds, approvals, recipient basis, and public obligations within its mandate. The government depository institution retains banking, custody, account, compliance, risk, security, operations, settlement, evidence, reconciliation, and production responsibilities within its role. Technology providers do not absorb either institution.

## 10. Public-Finance Workflow Domains

The Program may support a range of public-finance domains when the responsible authorities approve the arrangement.

### Collections

LGU and agency collections may require payer attribution, purpose, reference, account mapping, channel evidence, posting, exception handling, refunds or corrections, reporting, and reconciliation.

### Assistance and social protection

LGU assistance, national social-protection activity, disaster response, and other beneficiary programs may require approved recipient bases, eligibility evidence, funding authority, release conditions, supported channels, exception resolution, and proof of outcome.

### Public payroll

Government payroll may require recurring authority, personnel attribution, account maintenance, timing, approvals, failed-credit treatment, reporting, and reconciliation across large participant groups.

### Pensions and benefits

Pension and benefit workflows may involve recurring entitlement, identity, life-status or other applicable evidence, account choice, practical access, unresolved payments, and support for vulnerable recipients. The responsible institution determines the lawful and permitted arrangement.

### Veterans-related benefits

Veterans-related activity may be relevant to institutions whose authority, relationships, and service design are established through research and agreement. The Program makes no institution-specific claim or recommendation at foundation stage.

### Scholarships and education support

Scholarship and education programs may require release by cohort, institution, semester, milestone, eligibility, or documentary condition, together with exceptions and continuing evidence.

### Agriculture and rural support

Agricultural assistance, grants, insurance-related proceeds, input support, and development programs may require geographically dispersed delivery, seasonal timing, participant validation, channel choice, and evidence suited to local operating conditions.

### Grants and government programs

Government grants and agency programs may require controlled releases, milestones, multiple participants, purpose restrictions, provider coordination, and transaction-to-program reconciliation.

### GOCC and public-institution operations

GOCCs and other public institutions may require collections, payroll, supplier settlement, customer disbursement, treasury coordination, or other workflows governed by their own authority and operating context.

### Procurement settlement

Approved supplier and procurement settlement may require contract reference, delivery or acceptance evidence, approval segregation, tax and accounting treatment, exception handling, and reconciliation. The platform does not replace procurement authority or legal review.

### Treasury workflows

Treasury-related activity may include funding recognition, liquidity visibility, reservations, release scheduling, provider balances, unresolved positions, reconciliation, and reporting. Canonical Treasury remains an operational view, not the legal account or accounting ledger.

### National-agency and inter-agency activity

National programs and inter-agency financial activity may require common authority across multiple operating participants, geographic scale, coordinated evidence, provider diversity, and durable records across institutional boundaries.

### Cross-border public obligations

Cross-border public obligations may become relevant for overseas beneficiaries, procurement, assistance, or other lawful purposes. Currency, foreign-exchange, sanctions, compliance, settlement, provider, data, and legal requirements must be established before use.

This taxonomy defines possible workflow domains. It does not imply that any named institution possesses authority or readiness for them. [Annex C](annexes/workflow-taxonomy.md) supplies the complete classification and assessment fields.

## 12. Commercial Architecture

The commercial architecture separates the right to use maintained software from institution-specific work and regulated financial services.

### Software license

The x-change software license governs the institution's approved right to use maintained software within defined scope. It does not include banking authority, government-depository authority, settlement access, or a guarantee of public-sector business.

### Implementation and integration

Institution-specific discovery, configuration, driver mapping and engineering, system integration, testing, deployment support, operating preparation, and launch work require an approved scope, responsibilities, dependencies, acceptance criteria, and commercial instrument.

### Technical support and maintenance

Maintenance may include supported releases, defect correction, security remediation, compatibility, documentation, test capability, and agreed evolution. Institution-specific driver maintenance remains a distinct responsibility within the agreed support boundary.

### Delegated DevOps or managed operations

Managed deployment or operations are separately delegated services. Their scope, access, service levels, security responsibilities, change authority, incident obligations, audit rights, subcontracting, continuity, and exit arrangements require their own governance and commercial treatment.

### Settlement-provider fees

Account, network, transaction, switching, sponsor, foreign-exchange, and other regulated-provider charges remain governed by the depository institution's provider relationships and applicable program arrangements.

### Program-specific services

Optional services may include workflow discovery, program configuration, data migration, training, operating design, evidence design, reconciliation support, analytics, or other approved work. These must not be implied as part of the base license when they are separately scoped.

### Institution-specific proposal

Pricing, taxes, payment terms, validity, volumes, service levels, implementation assumptions, liabilities, and legal terms belong in a separately authorized institution-specific proposal and agreement. This Program foundation invents none.

Deposits and transaction activity may follow successful public-finance relationships. They are not license outputs. Financial value must be evaluated from the actual workflow, authority, program structure, pricing, operating cost, provider charges, risk, service obligations, and performance.

## 14. Submission Architecture

Future institution-specific submissions will use the existing [Submission Portfolio Architecture](../../submissions/SUBMISSION_ARCHITECTURE.md) and [Submission Grammar](../../submissions/SUBMISSION_GRAMMAR.md).

The intended interface is conceptually equivalent to:

```text
publish:submission
    --program=government-depository
    --institution=<institution-slug>
```

The present repository uses a manifest-driven Python builder. A future implementation should extend that builder or add a thin program/institution selection layer. It must not duplicate the AllBank packaging logic.

An institution manifest will declare approved recipients, source artifacts, order, screen and print profiles, expected versions, checksums, page counts, QR verification where applicable, archive identity, and output location.

A submission may contain only approved institution-specific artifacts. The builder must copy approved PDFs byte-for-byte, generate packaging derivatives separately, verify source and packaged checksums, produce a deterministic archive, verify extraction equivalence, and preserve the frozen state of included publications.

## 15. Publication Governance

The Program follows the repository's established publishing governance.

### Canonical Markdown

Markdown remains the source of truth for prose, specifications, governance, and institution-specific manuscripts. Generated PDFs, HTML, images, and archives do not replace the canonical source.

### Metadata

YAML records publication identity, form, version, status, audience, lineage, source paths, governance, publication outputs, and institution-specific boundaries. Metadata must not claim a build, lock, or approval that has not occurred.

### Renderer-generated outputs

When publication is authorized, existing Program Guide styles, typography, PDF profiles, metadata handling, bookmarks, internal links, and page QA should be reused. A different public-finance context does not justify a new house style or renderer.

### Deterministic builds and checksums

Publication and submission builders should validate canonical hashes, generate deterministic outputs where supported, record artifact checksums, and verify reproducibility across consecutive builds.

### Freeze records and version history

An accepted publication receives an explicit freeze record defining its source, edition, version, canonical assets, build command, checksums, and future versioning policy. A frozen edition is not overwritten.

### Source-to-artifact traceability

Every publication artifact must identify its canonical source, renderer or builder, edition, version, and checksum. Packaging derivatives must remain distinguishable from source publications.

### Visual inspection

Automated checks do not replace page-by-page visual review. Publications must be inspected for clipping, overflow, broken tables, malformed diagrams, widows, orphans, page-numbering defects, navigation, metadata, and print suitability.

### Preservation of frozen publications

Movement IV, approved Rural and Thrift publications, the Executive Demonstration Credential Version 1.0, and the AllBank Executive Submission Portfolio Version 1.0 remain unchanged. The Government Depository Program reuses their governance patterns without revising their baselines.

## 16. Program Grammar

The canonical lifecycle is:

```text
Program
   ↓
Research
   ↓
Institution Assessment
   ↓
Institution Strategy
   ↓
Executive Publications
   ↓
Commercial Proposal
   ↓
Demonstration or POC Package
   ↓
Submission Portfolio
   ↓
Freeze
   ↓
Version History
```

### Program

The sector-level Program defines purpose, doctrine, architecture, institutional field, stakeholder model, workflow taxonomy, commercial categories, publication system, adoption path, risks, and readiness gates. It contains no institution-specific conclusion.

### Research

Institution Intelligence establishes attributable facts, bounded inferences, recommendations, and unknowns under the canonical evidence policy.

### Institution Assessment

Strategic Gap Analysis compares accepted institutional evidence with the Program and identifies alignment, unknowns, dependencies, and questions without declaring the institution deficient.

### Institution Strategy

Editorial Mapping and the Institution Edition translate the Program into institutionally relevant language and examples without changing doctrine.

### Executive Publications

Approved briefs and editions support Board, management, government, control-function, and partner understanding. They remain traceable to the Program and evidence base.

### Commercial Proposal

A proposal defines offered scope, responsibilities, assumptions, dependencies, exclusions, commercial structure, and decision gates. It does not rewrite research or authorize production by itself.

### Demonstration or POC Package

A controlled demonstration or Proof of Concept tests defined behavior or supports institutional understanding. Technical success is not operational readiness, public authority, or production approval.

### Submission Portfolio

A governed manifest assembles approved publications for delivery. Packaging does not create new editions.

### Freeze and Version History

Accepted editions become immutable baselines. Later changes proceed through explicit versioning and visible supersession.

Sector-level artifacts and institution-specific artifacts must remain separate. A sector-level Program cannot state what a named institution intends, operates, or should prioritize. An institution-specific artifact cannot redefine the sector-level platform grammar.

## 17. Adoption Roadmap

Institutional adoption proceeds through evidence, not presumption.

### Discovery

Confirm the institution, public entity, candidate workflow, applicable authorities, participants, accounts, data, operating context, providers, evidence needs, constraints, and material unknowns.

### Authority and suitability assessment

Determine whether the institution and government entity possess the required authority and whether the candidate arrangement is suitable for further evaluation. Legal, regulatory, procurement, charter, policy, fund-characterization, accounting, data, and institutional questions remain with the responsible authorities.

### Workflow selection

Choose one workflow important enough to justify institutional attention and bounded enough to govern. Define purpose, participants, authority, funding, controls, conditions, channels, settlement, evidence, support, exceptions, reconciliation, and accountable owners.

### Controlled demonstration

Use an approved controlled environment to establish whether the proposed ceremony and integrations can be represented and observed. A demonstration may use governed experiential artifacts. It must remain visibly non-production.

### Institutional review

The responsible government and banking functions examine architecture, security, data, operations, risk, compliance, legal, Treasury, accounting, reconciliation, support, continuity, accessibility, provider, and public-accountability evidence.

### Proof of Concept or pilot

A POC tests an approved technical or operating scope. A production pilot, if separately authorized, operates within defined participants, values, duration, channels, evidence, support, escalation, and decision authority.

### Production qualification

Production may proceed only when authority, agreements, procurement or engagement, integration, security, operating procedures, personnel, support, accounting, reconciliation, continuity, provider readiness, and institutional approvals are complete to the responsible institution's standard.

### Modular expansion

After evidence review, the institution may Proceed, Correct, Extend, or Pause. Expansion may introduce additional participants, locations, channels, limits, providers, or workflows. It is never automatic.

## 18. Program Risks

| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal and regulatory uncertainty | Authority may differ by institution, government entity, fund, workflow, and period | Record authority questions; obtain responsible legal and regulatory review before commitment |
| Institutional eligibility | Depository status does not establish qualification for every program | Separate eligibility, qualification, selection, agreement, and readiness |
| Procurement and engagement | Public-sector participation may require specific procurement or partnership processes | Treat process and authority as explicit dependencies |
| Fund characterization | Software state must not determine ownership, appropriation, trust, deposit, or accounting character | Preserve legal and accounting treatment outside canonical operational state |

| Cybersecurity | Compromise can affect funds, service continuity, evidence, and public confidence | Apply institution-controlled security, segregation, testing, monitoring, incident response, and recovery |

| Reconciliation | Unresolved or ambiguous outcomes can become public obligations and audit issues | Define transaction-level evidence, status, exception ownership, aging, correction, and closure |

| Public accountability | Speed or automation can obscure authority and responsibility | Preserve traceable approvals, roles, evidence, exceptions, and institutional memory |
| Unsupported claims | Premature claims can misstate institutional authority, capability, or intent | Apply evidence classification and retain Unknown where evidence is absent |
| Architectural drift | Sector-specific language may quietly redefine Pay Code, Treasury, custody, or settlement | Apply canonical parity review and the Agent Balk Rule |
| Publication drift | Derivatives may become inconsistent sources of doctrine | Maintain source hierarchy, metadata, checksums, freeze records, and version history |

Risk management remains institution- and workflow-specific. This register defines the program-level questions that future work must answer; it is not a legal, security, or risk assessment.

## 19. Definition of Done

The Program foundation is mature enough for institution-specific implementation work only when all of the following conditions are satisfied.

### Canonical foundation

- the Program purpose, vision, institutional scope, product, outcomes, architecture, operating model, stakeholder model, commercial logic, publication architecture, adoption path, and risks are approved;
- the canonical x-change, Treasury, execution, custody, settlement, licensing, and operating-sovereignty doctrines remain unchanged;
- the program annexes contain no placeholders; and
- the parity report identifies rather than conceals missing publication assets.

### Formal publication layer

- the Government Depository Program Guide implements the EDR-005 Executive Summary, Sections 1–7, and Appendices A–G architecture;
- all 27 mandatory coverage categories and business questions are complete;
- program diagrams reuse or explicitly extend approved canonical concepts without divergence;
- publication metadata, renderer, navigation, checksums, QA, freeze, and version history are complete; and
- executive, technical, commercial, stakeholder, and financial derivatives remain traceable to approved sources.

### Institution-research gate

- the target institution has an authorized research workstream;
- Institution Intelligence distinguishes Observed, Inferred, Recommended, Unknown, and Institution-Provided material;
- material authority, ownership, governance, strategy, financial, public-finance, product, channel, technology, settlement, and relationship questions have been researched to the applicable gate;
- unsupported claims and unresolved matters remain visible; and
- the Strategic Gap Analysis and Editorial Mapping gates are satisfied before an Institution Edition is drafted.

### Implementation gate

- the candidate workflow, government entity, authorities, participants, accounts, funds, data, provider relationships, evidence, reconciliation, controls, support, continuity, and responsible owners are known;
- the institution-specific driver requirement has passed discovery and mapping;
- commercial and legal instruments are approved for the proposed stage; and
- the responsible institutions, not a provider, determine whether sufficient evidence exists to proceed.

Completion of the Program foundation does not itself satisfy the formal publication, institution-research, or implementation gates.

The Philippine Veterans Bank workstream may begin only at the institution-research gate described in [Annex F](annexes/institution-specific-research-gate.md). [Annex G](annexes/definition-of-institution-readiness.md) defines the separate decision from sector documentation to institution assessment and from technical capability to institutional operation.
