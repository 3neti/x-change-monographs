# Annex F — Institution Intelligence Research Gate

## Purpose

This annex defines the minimum evidence and decision discipline required before the Government Depository Program may be adapted to a named institution.

It applies the locked [Institution Intelligence Framework](../../../institutions/FRAMEWORK.md), [Public Source Policy](../../../institutions/PUBLIC_SOURCE_POLICY.md), [Institution Intelligence Standard](../../../institutions/INSTITUTION_INTELLIGENCE_STANDARD.md), and [Editorial Mapping Standard](../../../institutions/EDITORIAL_MAPPING_STANDARD.md). It does not replace or amend them.

No Institution Edition, modernization strategy, commercial proposal, implementation roadmap, demonstration package, or submission portfolio should begin from sector assumptions.

## Governing Principle

> Institution-specific work begins with evidence, not manuscript writing.

The research question is not whether the institution can be made to fit x-change. It is:

> How does this institution relate to the approved Government Depository doctrine, based on attributable evidence?

## Canonical Evidence Classes

Every material institution-specific statement must remain identifiable as one of the following:

- **Observed** — directly supported by an identified source.
- **Inferred** — a bounded interpretation drawn from identified observations.
- **Recommended** — a proposed action, emphasis, ceremony, sequence, or application of doctrine.
- **Unknown** — material information not established by available evidence.
- **Institution-Provided** — information intentionally supplied by the institution and governed by its stated authority, access, and use conditions.

An observation does not make every inference correct. A recommendation does not become an institutional intention until the institution adopts it. An explicit Unknown is preferable to unsupported completion.

## Confidence Annotation

Evidence class and confidence answer different questions. The class describes what kind of statement is being made. Confidence describes how strongly the available evidence supports that statement for the stated period and scope.

| Confidence | Use | Typical basis | Required qualification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| High | Direct material claims | Current regulator or registry record, audited disclosure, applicable charter or official instrument, or clear institution-issued primary source | Preserve exact period, scope, definition, and limitations |
| Moderate | Supported but bounded claims | Direct source with limited scope or age; multiple consistent primary signals; corroborated institution statement | Explain the boundary and any unresolved contrary evidence |
| Low | Research leads and cautious interpretation | Indirect public signal, one secondary source, dated disclosure, job posting, product surface, or incomplete evidence | Do not present as current fact; retain alternatives and research need |
| Not established | Material point remains unknown | No reliable evidence, contradictory evidence without resolution, or inaccessible institution-only information | Classify as Unknown or Institution-Provided pending evidence |

Confidence belongs to a claim, not permanently to a source. An audited report may provide high confidence for a historical financial figure and no confidence about current API readiness.

## Source Hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order when they address the same claim:

1. regulator, official registry, law, charter, rule, or audited disclosure;
2. institution-issued report, filing, policy, product page, or announcement;
3. named statement by an authorized institutional representative;
4. primary documentation from an identified partner or provider;
5. reputable independent reporting; and
6. secondary commentary used only with appropriate caution.

A weaker source may identify a lead. It should not displace a stronger source that directly establishes the matter.

## Evidence Source Architecture

### Laws, charters, regulatory materials, and official records

These sources may establish institutional classification, authority, ownership, governance, prudential status, public mandate, procurement framework, depository rules, and other formal conditions.

They must be read within date and scope. Authority to perform a class of activity does not establish current capability, selection, or readiness for a specific workflow.

### Annual reports and audited financial statements

These may establish reporting-period financial position, business segments, ownership, governance, strategy, risk disclosures, channels, partnerships, and material initiatives.

Every financial figure must preserve period, currency, unit, reporting perimeter, definition, comparative period, and page. Calculated values must show the formula and cite every input.

An annual report is authoritative for what it discloses. It does not establish undisclosed systems, current implementation status after the reporting period, or management intent beyond the stated material.

### Public disclosures and institution websites

Official product pages, service notices, press releases, investor presentations, sustainability reports, branch lists, support pages, app descriptions, and release notes may show what the institution publicly offers or states.

A visible channel does not reveal the complete operating architecture beneath it. Product availability does not by itself establish adoption, profitability, technical ownership, API suitability, or strategic priority.

### Strategic plans, speeches, and management statements

Authorized plans, speeches, interviews, and presentations may establish stated priorities and management framing for a defined date.

Record the speaker, role, date, audience, context, and exact scope. A stated aspiration is not evidence of completed capability. A useful x-change application is not automatically an institutional priority.

### Public procurement, partnership, and provider records

Notices, awards, bid documents, agreements disclosed publicly, provider case studies, and partnership announcements may identify counterparties, scope signals, dates, and implementation claims.

They require careful attribution. A provider announcement may describe the provider's contribution without establishing the institution's complete architecture, production state, financial terms, or continuing relationship.

### Reputable independent reporting

Independent reporting may provide context, chronology, quotations, and leads. Record the publisher, author, date, attributed sources, and material limitations.

Use it cautiously for high-consequence claims and verify through a stronger source where practical.

### Public technology signals

Technology partnerships, certifications, public hiring, cloud references, named vendors, API pages, security programs, and developer materials are signals.

They do not, without further evidence, establish:

- production architecture;
- system ownership;
- technical maturity or implementation quality;
- security effectiveness;
- API availability to x-change;
- account or settlement structure;
- the existence or readiness of `3neti/emi-{institution}`; or
- willingness to adopt the Program.

### Institution-provided material

Institution-provided material may include interviews, questionnaires, internal presentations, policies, architecture documents, API specifications, commercial data, operating reports, account and provider structures, and implementation evidence.

Record:

- who supplied it and under what authority;
- date supplied and reporting period;
- confidentiality and distribution restrictions;
- whether it may be quoted, summarized, or used only for internal analysis;
- whether it is draft, approved, current, or superseded;
- conflicts with public evidence; and
- which downstream artifacts may use it.

Institution-provided material must remain distinguishable from public evidence.

## Required Source Record

Every material source must record:

- source ID;
- title;
- publisher or issuing institution;
- document or publication date;
- URL or stable repository reference;
- access date;
- reporting period;
- source type and hierarchy level;
- relevant pages or sections;
- claims supported;
- confidence contribution;
- material limitations or contradictions; and
- whether a lawful local evidence copy is retained.

Links alone are not research notes. The register must state what the source establishes and what it does not.

## Research Domains

### Institutional identity and authority

Research:

- history and institutional development;
- charter, amendments, classification, and relevant authority;
- ownership, group relationships, governance, Board, and executive leadership;
- mandate, constituencies, and publicly stated positioning;
- depository authority and evidenced government activity; and
- material regulatory, legal, or institutional constraints.

Distinguish the institution from parents, subsidiaries, affiliates, service providers, and sector associations.

### Strategy and institutional direction

Research explicit strategic priorities, transformation programs, target markets, public-sector positioning, partnerships, channel development, efficiency initiatives, and management commentary.

Use **Observed** for explicit priorities and **Inferred** for patterns. Do not assign an intention merely because a capability would be useful.

### Products, services, and public-finance activity

Assess evidenced offerings and activity relevant to:

- deposits and cash management;
- public and private payroll;
- collections;
- pensions and benefits;
- government assistance and disbursement;
- merchant and supplier services;
- treasury and settlement;
- cards, QR, Internet, mobile, branch, and partner channels;
- remittance and cross-border services;
- lending and public-sector relationships; and
- other public services.

Record whether an offering is current, announced, historical, partner-delivered, pilot, or uncertain.

### Financial and commercial profile

Research, where public and comparable:

- assets, deposits, loans, capital, liquidity, profitability, asset quality, and funding mix;
- public-sector deposits or transaction activity only where specifically disclosed;
- revenue and fee signals;
- cost and investment signals;
- ownership and capital constraints;
- disclosed provider, transformation, or technology commitments; and
- procurement and commercial patterns relevant to an authorized engagement.

Do not infer program economics from total bank figures. Do not invent pricing, volumes, revenue shares, or returns.

### Customer, constituency, and geographic profile

Research customer groups, public constituencies, branches, service locations, digital reach, regional presence, accessibility, and publicly evidenced relationships.

Branch count does not establish service quality or digital readiness. A statutory or historical constituency does not establish current product adoption.

### Technology assessment

Separate visible channels from operating capability.

Assess public and institution-provided evidence for:

- core and channel environment where disclosed;
- cloud, hosting, network, and resilience posture;
- engineering and release capability;
- identity, access, security, privacy, monitoring, and incident practice;
- API management and provider interfaces;
- data architecture and records;
- test environments and certification;
- service management, support, backup, and recovery; and
- outsourcing and DevOps arrangements.

The assessment should state **Observed**, **Inferred**, **Unknown**, and **Institution-Provided** separately. It must not produce a production architecture from public signals.

### Settlement and payments assessment

Research evidenced accounts, participation, rails, providers, network memberships, limits, credentials, settlement timing, reconciliation, exceptions, reversals, and provider dependencies.

Public payment capability does not establish the account structure or API behavior required by x-change. Driver discovery remains necessary.

### Public-finance operating assessment

Assess the institution's evidenced ability to relate:

- public authority and banking responsibility;
- government and participant data;
- funding and account structures;
- approvals and segregation;
- participant experience and support;
- execution and provider evidence;
- accounting and reconciliation;
- unresolved outcomes;
- continuity and incident response; and
- audit and regulatory evidence.

This is not a declaration of readiness. It identifies what evidence exists and what discovery remains.

### Architecture assessment

Compare available institution evidence with the canonical boundaries in Annex A:

- separate institution-controlled deployment;
- software license and maintenance boundary;
- institution-controlled data and credentials;
- `emi-core` common execution contract;
- institution-specific driver requirement;
- approved provider and settlement relationships;
- custody and authority boundaries;
- evidence and reconciliation; and
- readiness as an institutional judgment.

If institution evidence appears to require new doctrine, stop at the conflict and identify whether an Editorial Decision Record is required.

## Institution Interviews

Interviews are structured evidence-gathering sessions, not opportunities to convert informal discussion into unqualified fact.

### Interview preparation

Prepare:

- the research question and evidence gaps;
- participant names, roles, and expected authority;
- source-backed observations to confirm or challenge;
- questions separated by governance, business, operations, Treasury, Finance, Technology, Security, Risk, Compliance, Legal, Audit, and provider domain;
- confidentiality and recording arrangements; and
- a method for review or confirmation of material notes.

### Interview record

Record:

- date, participants, roles, and purpose;
- whether statements are attributable, non-attributable, confidential, or restricted;
- the speaker for every material statement;
- documents or evidence promised;
- contradictions with public sources;
- decisions, opinions, intentions, and unknowns separately; and
- required follow-up.

An interview statement should be classified **Institution-Provided** unless it is independently supported as Observed public evidence.

### Interview limitations

One executive's statement may not represent formal institutional approval. One technical discussion may not establish production architecture. A commercial interest may not establish authority or readiness. Record the speaker's role and scope.

## Research Workflow

1. **Authorize scope** — Define institution, research question, audience, evidence cutoff, public-source basis, and restrictions.
2. **Create source plan** — Identify primary sources, regulatory materials, institution disclosures, interviews, and likely evidence gaps.
3. **Build source register** — Record provenance, period, authority, claims, and limitations.
4. **Extract claim-level evidence** — Capture exact observations with page or section references.
5. **Classify and assess confidence** — Separate Observed, Inferred, Recommended, Unknown, and Institution-Provided material; annotate confidence.
6. **Resolve or record contradictions** — Prefer stronger evidence only with stated reasoning.
7. **Conduct gap-led interviews** — Ask for institution evidence where public sources cannot establish material matters.
8. **Complete Institution Intelligence** — Describe the institution before proposing what it should do.
9. **Perform Strategic Gap Analysis** — Compare accepted evidence with approved Program doctrine.
10. **Prepare Editorial Mapping** — Authorize institution-specific narrative, examples, ceremonies, and claims.
11. **Decide writing readiness** — Do not begin an Institution Edition until the mapping permits it.

## Required Institution Intelligence Package

The package should contain:

- research brief and authorization;
- institution metadata and evidence cutoff;
- public source register;
- evidence register;
- interview register and permitted-use notes;
- Institution Intelligence report;
- financial tables with periods, units, definitions, and sources;
- product and channel inventory;
- technology, settlement, and architecture signal register;
- contradictions and unknowns register;
- questions for management;
- Strategic Gap Analysis; and
- Editorial Mapping when the preceding gates permit it.

## Editorial Mapping Gate

Editorial Mapping must identify:

- canonical parent Guide and doctrinal source;
- institution-specific thesis supported by evidence;
- protected doctrine;
- permitted adaptations;
- audience and decision purpose;
- section-by-section narrative mapping;
- selected ceremonies and evidentiary basis;
- claim and evidence map;
- terminology map;
- inclusion and exclusion register;
- visual and evidence brief;
- open questions; and
- drafting authorization.

Permitted drafting statuses are:

- **Not Ready for Drafting**;
- **Ready for Limited Drafting**;
- **Ready for Drafting**; or
- **Deviation Decision Required**.

## Prohibited Shortcuts

The following do not satisfy the gate:

- relying on Movement IV's intended first-reader positioning as evidence of institutional commitment;
- treating charter authority as proof of present operating capability;
- treating a website product description as proof of implementation readiness;
- treating payment connectivity as a complete banking service;
- inferring government relationships, customer counts, architecture, APIs, account structures, priorities, or pricing without evidence;
- treating job postings or vendor announcements as proof of deployed capability;
- copying facts or claims from another institution's edition;
- blending public and institution-provided evidence without attribution;
- converting interview interest into institutional approval;
- drafting a proposal before institutional, legal, technical, operational, and commercial questions have been established; or
- using polished prose to conceal Unknown.

## Gate Decisions

### Evidence readiness

Institution Intelligence may enter review only when material claims have attributable sources, dates and periods are visible, statement classes are distinguishable, contradictions and Unknowns are recorded, and source limitations are disclosed.

### Analytical readiness

Strategic Gap Analysis may begin only from reviewed Institution Intelligence and must cite both institution evidence and the canonical doctrine used for comparison.

### Editorial readiness

Editorial Mapping may begin only after principal evidence and gap findings have been reviewed. Unresolved matters remain visible.

### Writing readiness

An Institution Edition may begin only from an accepted Editorial Mapping. It may adapt approved examples and emphasis. It may not create new doctrine.

## Gate Outcome

The research gate may produce:

- **Proceed to analysis**;
- **Proceed with stated Unknowns**;
- **Pause for institution-provided evidence**;
- **Deviation decision required**; or
- **Do not proceed**.

It must never manufacture certainty merely to advance the publication sequence.
