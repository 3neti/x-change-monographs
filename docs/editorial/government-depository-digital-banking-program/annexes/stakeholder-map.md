# Annex B — Stakeholder Map

## Purpose

This map identifies the stakeholder classes that a Government Depository Digital Banking Program must consider. It does not assign statutory authority, establish a procurement relationship, or represent the organization of a specific institution.

## Stakeholder Classes

| Stakeholder class | Principal interest | Boundary to preserve | Typical interaction with the Program |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Executive and legislative authorities | Lawful, effective, and accountable use of public money | Policy and statutory authority cannot be delegated to software | Define or oversee public purpose, authority, and institutional mandate |
| Finance, budget, and treasury authorities | Budget execution, cash management, control, reporting, and fiscal discipline | Public-finance rules and approvals remain authoritative | Set conditions for funding, accounts, reporting, reconciliation, and control |
| Procuring and implementing government entities | Reliable delivery of an authorized collection or disbursement workflow | Service configuration cannot replace procurement, program, or administrative authority | Define the service, participants, evidence, exceptions, and performance expectations |
| Government depository institutions | Trust, custody, banking authority, operations, risk, settlement, reconciliation, and customer or agency relationships | Each institution remains independent and accountable for its own operations | Provide the banking service and operate the approved capability |
| Oversight, audit, and regulatory bodies | Legality, prudence, traceability, records, consumer protection, and control effectiveness | Oversight independence and regulatory authority remain intact | Examine authority, design, execution evidence, exceptions, and institutional readiness |
| Beneficiaries, payees, taxpayers, employers, merchants, and other participants | Clear access, correct value, fair treatment, privacy, recourse, and completion | Participation rights and obligations cannot be inferred from a technical credential | Use or receive the authorized banking service and its evidence |
| Settlement banks, payment systems, and financial-service providers | Accurate instructions, authorized accounts, operational reliability, and defined liabilities | Connectivity does not confer public authority or create the banking service | Execute a regulated or contracted portion of the service |
| x-change licensor | Integrity and maintainability of the canonical operating grammar and licensed software | Does not become the bank, custodian, public authority, or settlement provider | Licenses and maintains the common software foundation |
| DevOps and implementation providers | Secure delivery, observable operation, support, and controlled change | Technical administration does not transfer institutional authority or production accountability | Build, deploy, operate, and support within delegated boundaries |
| Internal audit, compliance, risk, legal, security, operations, and technology teams | Institutional control, defensibility, continuity, and readiness | No single technical milestone substitutes for collective institutional approval | Assess, govern, approve, monitor, and evidence the operating model |

## Stakeholder Questions

Every future Program Guide, Institution Edition, and proposal should answer, at the appropriate level:

1. Who possesses the authority to establish the public purpose and workflow?
2. Which institution provides the banking service?
3. Who owns or controls the relevant accounts, funds, data, infrastructure, credentials, and approvals?
4. Which provider performs each regulated, technical, or operational function?
5. Who approves readiness and production use?
6. Who receives, reconciles, preserves, and reviews execution evidence?
7. Who manages exceptions, complaints, reversals, continuity events, and control failures?
8. What remains unknown or dependent on institution-provided evidence?

## Stakeholder-Briefing Rule

A common architecture does not justify a common explanation. Board members, finance and treasury officials, operations leaders, technology teams, auditors, regulators, and public participants require different levels of detail. Future stakeholder briefs may adapt language and emphasis, but must remain traceable to the same approved Program doctrine and must not alter responsibility boundaries.
