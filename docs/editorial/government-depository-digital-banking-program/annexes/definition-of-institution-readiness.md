# Annex G — Institution Readiness

## Purpose

This annex defines the maturity model through which the Government Depository Program progresses from sector doctrine to institution research, Program authorization, implementation, controlled production, and expansion.

Readiness is not one technical state. It is a sequence of evidence-based decisions made by institutions responsible for public authority, banking, risk, operations, technology, finance, oversight, and public accountability.

## Readiness Principle

> Readiness is an institutional judgment, not a technical declaration.

Software availability, a successful demonstration, API access, driver certification, provider onboarding, or Board interest may each be necessary. None is sufficient by itself.

## Maturity Model

```text
Sector Readiness
        ↓
Institution Research Readiness
        ↓
Institution Assessment Readiness
        ↓
Program Readiness
        ↓
Implementation Readiness
        ↓
Driver Readiness
        ↓
Operational Readiness
        ↓
Limited-Production Readiness
        ↓
Production Readiness
        ↓
Expansion Readiness
```

The sequence is directional, not automatic. A later concern may reveal that an earlier decision must be revisited.

| Publication readiness | Does the Program Guide answer every EDR-005 business question and mandatory coverage category? | Reviewed Executive Summary, Sections 1–7, canonical Guide Appendices A–G, parity, metadata, QA, acceptance, and freeze decision | Editorial governance | Publish an approved edition; use it as the sector implementation source |

| Expansion readiness | Should the institution add a workflow, participant group, geography, channel, provider, value, or volume? | Evidence from current operation, capacity and risk assessment, affected authority, change design, regression evidence, and approval | Responsible institutional authorities | Extend only the approved dimension; preserve continuing review |

## Sector Readiness

Sector readiness establishes that the Program is coherent, governed, and architecturally aligned.

### Criteria

- Movement IV supplies approved institutional doctrine.
- The Program Foundation defines purpose, institutional scope, product, architecture, workflows, commercial boundaries, publication system, risks, and research gate.
- Canonical x-change, Treasury, custody, settlement, licensing, and operating-sovereignty doctrine remains unchanged.
- The Program Guide implements EDR-005 without silently missing a business question.
- Supporting and Guide appendices are complete and correctly distinguished.
- Publication, research, and institution-specific work remain separate layers.

Sector readiness does not establish that any named institution is suitable or ready.

## Institution Readiness

Institution readiness begins with evidence about the institution itself.

### Research criteria

- the research workstream is authorized;
- primary and secondary sources are recorded;
- Observed, Inferred, Recommended, Unknown, and Institution-Provided statements remain distinct;
- authority, ownership, governance, strategy, products, finances, channels, technology, settlement, public-finance activity, and constraints are sufficiently examined;
- material contradictions and stale evidence are visible; and
- questions requiring institution discovery are identified.

### Assessment criteria

- Institution Intelligence is accepted for analysis;
- Strategic Gap Analysis compares evidence with canonical doctrine;
- the assessment does not label absence of public evidence as institutional deficiency;
- technology signals are not presented as production architecture; and
- the institution's possible fit is distinguished from a recommendation or commitment.

### Editorial criteria

- Editorial Mapping identifies protected doctrine and permitted adaptations;
- every material institution-specific claim has evidence and approved wording;
- representative ceremonies have a documented institutional basis;
- Unknowns and exclusions remain visible; and
- drafting authorization is explicit.

## Program Readiness

Program readiness answers whether the institution should begin a governed evaluation—not whether it should enter production.

### Required evidence

- institutional sponsor and decision authority;
- statement of institutional purpose;
- relationship to charter, mandate, strategy, and risk appetite;
- initial public-finance opportunity or research question;
- preliminary authority and suitability assessment;
- governance and participating functions;
- approved boundaries between public entity, AGDB, providers, and software;
- resources for discovery; and
- explicit next decision.

### Board distinction

Board approval begins the Program. It does not:

- approve a particular government program;
- confer depository authority or eligibility;
- complete procurement or contracting;
- certify an API or provider;
- approve an account or fund structure;
- establish operational readiness; or
- place the institution into production.

## Workflow Readiness

The first ceremony should be institutionally important and operationally bounded.

It is ready for detailed design when the parties can identify:

- public purpose and authority;
- responsible public entity and banking institution;
- source and character of funds;
- accounts and custody;
- participants and authoritative data;
- banking service and Facility owner;
- conditions and evidence;
- channels and presentation;
- execution and settlement path;
- support, complaints, exceptions, and reversals;
- accounting and reconciliation; and
- accountable decision owners.

If these cannot be made explicit, the ceremony is not ready merely because a technical demo is possible.

## Implementation Readiness

Implementation readiness establishes that engineering and operating preparation can begin responsibly.

### Required package

- approved scope and success criteria;
- architecture and responsibility records;
- data inventory, flows, authority, privacy, and records treatment;
- institution-controlled environment and access plan;
- CI/CD, release, monitoring, backup, recovery, and incident plan;
- API and provider discovery plan;
- driver scope and maintenance responsibilities;
- test environments, cases, evidence, and acceptance;
- operating, support, accounting, and reconciliation design;
- legal, procurement, commercial, and outsourcing instruments appropriate to the stage; and
- project governance and unresolved-dependency register.

Implementation readiness authorizes controlled work. It is not a commitment to production.

## Driver Readiness

The driver is ready for complete-ceremony validation when evidence shows that it can perform the agreed technical behavior for the approved scope.

### Technical criteria

- endpoints, messages, schemas, and versions are mapped;
- authentication, certificates, signatures, credentials, and rotation are tested;
- idempotency and duplicate handling are evidenced;
- synchronous and asynchronous statuses are mapped;
- webhooks, polling, timeouts, retries, and ambiguity are handled;
- limits, validation, errors, reversals, and cancellations are tested;
- provider and institution references remain traceable;
- reconciliation evidence is complete enough for the approved model;
- security and performance expectations are met;
- deployment and rollback are controlled; and
- ownership, support, change notice, and maintenance are defined.

### Driver decision

Possible decisions are:

- **Not ready**;
- **Ready for further technical testing**;
- **Certified for the defined test scope**; or
- **Certified for complete-ceremony validation**.

No driver decision is operational or production readiness.

## Operational Readiness

Operational readiness examines the complete ceremony.

### Operating dimensions

| Dimension | Evidence required |
| --- | --- |
| Authority and governance | Current approvals, accountable owners, segregation, escalation, and decision rights |
| Funds and accounts | Approved funding, account structure, custody, limits, timing, and reconciliation ownership |
| Participants and data | Authoritative sources, identity, quality, correction, privacy, access, retention, and records |
| Service and conditions | Facility, Settlement Envelope, participant journey, communication, and support |
| Execution | Driver, provider, credentials, statuses, limits, ambiguity, failure, and reversal behavior |
| Operations | Procedures, staffing, training, service windows, monitoring, support, complaints, and escalation |
| Finance and reconciliation | Accounting treatment, provider charges, references, cutoffs, unresolved items, aging, and closure |
| Security and resilience | Access, testing, monitoring, incident response, backup, recovery, continuity, and alternatives |
| Evidence and oversight | Records, audit trail, reporting, control evidence, regulator or auditor needs, and retention |

### Complete-ceremony tests

Test at minimum:

- successful authorized execution;
- invalid or incomplete authority;
- insufficient or unavailable operational resources;
- duplicate instruction;
- delayed or missing provider response;
- ambiguous outcome;
- partial batch or participant failure;
- expired or invalid credential;
- incorrect participant or account data;
- reversal, correction, or refund where applicable;
- channel, provider, or system outage;
- credential compromise or unauthorized access scenario;
- reconciliation difference; and
- evidence retrieval and audit trace.

The test result must show not only what the software did, but what the responsible people and institutions did next.

## Limited-Production Readiness

A pilot should be deliberately narrow and reversible within the approved governance.

### Pilot controls

- defined public entity and workflow;
- approved participant group;
- value, volume, geography, channel, and time limits;
- confirmed funding and provider capacity;
- named operating and escalation teams;
- enhanced monitoring and reconciliation;
- participant communication and support;
- security and incident response;
- pause, rollback, correction, and termination criteria;
- pre-agreed success measures; and
- evidence-review date and decision authority.

### Success evidence

Success measures may include:

- correct authority and approval;
- correct participant or payer attribution;
- execution outcome and timeliness;
- reconciliation completeness and timeliness;
- unresolved-item aging and closure;
- service availability and continuity;
- participant evidence and support performance;
- security and control effectiveness; and
- accuracy of public and banking reporting.

Volume alone is not readiness evidence.

## Production Readiness

Production readiness exists when the responsible institutions decide that the service can continue as an accountable operating responsibility.

### Transition criteria

- pilot evidence meets approved criteria or unresolved deviations are consciously accepted;
- authority, agreements, procurement or engagement, and account arrangements are complete;
- the institution has sufficient people, capacity, support, and provider commitments;
- financial, security, legal, risk, compliance, privacy, audit, and continuity decisions are complete;
- reconciliation and unresolved-item ownership are effective;
- monitoring, reporting, service review, and change governance are established; and
- the accountable authority records the production decision and approved scope.

Production approval applies only to the defined service. It does not authorize every workflow supported by the platform.

## Expansion Readiness

Expansion is a new responsibility decision, not an automatic reward for successful production.

An expansion may affect:

- public entity;
- workflow or Facility;
- participant population;
- geography;
- channel or presentation;
- provider or settlement route;
- value, volume, or operating window;
- conditions and evidence;
- data source;
- account structure; or
- support and continuity model.

The institution should assess what changes, what remains governed, which prior evidence remains valid, which tests must be repeated, and which authorities must approve.

## Decision Vocabulary

At every material gate, use a clear decision:

- **Proceed** — Evidence supports the defined next step.
- **Correct** — Identified deficiencies must be resolved before or during a controlled next step.
- **Extend** — Evidence supports a specifically bounded expansion.
- **Pause** — Material evidence, authority, capacity, or control is insufficient.
- **Stop** — The proposed path is unsuitable or unauthorized under the current basis.
- **Deviation decision required** — The proposed path appears to conflict with canonical doctrine or governance.

The decision should record scope, evidence, conditions, accountable owner, expiry or review date, and unresolved matters.

## No-Skipping Rules

- Publication completeness does not establish institution readiness.
- Institution interest does not establish Program authorization.
- Program authorization does not establish workflow authority.
- Workflow importance does not establish implementation readiness.
- API access does not establish driver readiness.
- Driver certification does not establish operational readiness.
- Operational testing does not establish public-program approval.
- A successful demonstration is not production evidence.
- A successful pilot does not authorize unrelated expansion.
- Provider assurance does not replace institutional judgment.

## Current Government Depository Status

The Program Foundation is approved. The Government Depository Program Guide remains Version 0.9 under editorial review and is not frozen or published.

Sector documentation does not establish readiness for a named institution. Philippine Veterans Bank has not been assessed by this annex or by the Program Guide. No statement in the Program sources should be interpreted as a finding that the bank is research-ready, Program-ready, technically ready, operationally ready, selected, or authorized for implementation.

The next institution-specific stage, when separately authorized, is Institution Intelligence under Annex F—not proposal writing or production design.
