# Annex C — Workflow Taxonomy

## Purpose

This taxonomy organizes representative public-finance workflows for future evaluation. Inclusion does not establish authority, eligibility, procurement preference, technical readiness, or a commitment to offer the workflow.

## Workflow Domains

| Domain | Representative ceremonies | Primary institutional concern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Government collections | Taxes, fees, permits, licenses, assessments, public-service payments, and accountable receipts | Correct attribution, authorized acceptance, timely recognition, and reconciliation |
| Payroll and workforce payments | Salaries, allowances, honoraria, reimbursements, and controlled employee benefits | Authorized payroll basis, participant identity, timing, exceptions, and evidence |
| Social protection and public assistance | Cash assistance, conditional benefits, emergency aid, and targeted subsidies | Eligibility, purpose, accessibility, leakage prevention, exception handling, and auditability |
| Pensions, veterans, and statutory benefits | Pensions, survivor benefits, reimbursements, and related authorized payments | Continuing eligibility, dignity of access, identity, continuity, and evidentiary control |
| Education and scholarship support | Scholarships, stipends, grants, tuition support, and institutional payments | Eligibility, milestones, purpose restrictions, disbursement evidence, and reconciliation |
| Agriculture, livelihood, and disaster response | Input support, crop or livelihood assistance, disaster relief, recovery grants, and local distribution | Speed with control, geography, participant validation, field accessibility, and post-event evidence |
| Procurement and government-enterprise payments | Supplier payments, contract milestones, grants to implementing entities, and government-owned enterprise workflows | Contract authority, milestone evidence, segregation of duties, and payment completion |
| Treasury and inter-account operations | Funding, allocation, controlled movement, settlement, reconciliation, and liquidity administration | Cash authority, account structure, timing, ledger segregation, and fund characterization |
| National and inter-agency programs | Shared or delegated programs involving multiple responsible public entities | Clear authority, institutional roles, data boundaries, reconciliation, and consolidated evidence |
| Cross-border public payments | Authorized remittances, pensions, aid, procurement, or other international public obligations | Foreign-exchange authority, sanctions and financial-crime controls, jurisdiction, settlement, and evidence |

## Workflow Assessment Frame

Each proposed workflow should be assessed through the same questions:

- **Authority:** What lawful and institutional authority permits the workflow?
- **Purpose:** What public result is the money intended to achieve?
- **Participants:** Who instructs, approves, funds, administers, receives, settles, reconciles, and oversees?
- **Funds and accounts:** Where do funds remain, how are they characterized, and what account structure applies?
- **Banking service:** What service does the depository institution provide?
- **Ceremony:** What does each participant actually do and observe?
- **Controls:** What eligibility, approval, limit, timing, purpose, and exception rules apply?
- **Presentation:** How is the credential or service presented without altering its canonical meaning?
- **Execution:** Which approved system and provider complete the instruction?
- **Evidence:** What must return to support accounting, participant recourse, reconciliation, oversight, and audit?
- **Continuity:** What happens when a participant, system, provider, channel, or settlement path is unavailable?
- **Readiness:** Which institution makes each readiness decision and on what evidence?

## Progression Rule

The Program should begin with one bounded ceremony whose authority, participants, funds, evidence, and operating responsibilities can be made explicit. Expansion should reuse the governed foundation and occur only after institutional evidence supports the next decision.
