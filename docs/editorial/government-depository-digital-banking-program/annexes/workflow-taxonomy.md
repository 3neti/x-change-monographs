# Annex C — Government Workflow Taxonomy

## Purpose

This taxonomy organizes representative public-finance workflows and shows how each may be examined through the canonical operating grammar. It is an evaluation reference, not a catalogue of authorized services.

Inclusion does not establish institutional authority, eligibility, procurement preference, technical readiness, commercial viability, or a commitment to implement the workflow.

## Common Assessment Frame

Every workflow should answer the same questions before implementation:

- **Authority:** What lawful and institutional authority permits the activity?
- **Purpose:** What public result is the money intended to achieve?
- **Participants:** Who instructs, approves, funds, administers, receives, settles, reconciles, and oversees?
- **Funds and accounts:** Where do funds remain, how are they characterized, and what account structure applies?
- **Banking service:** What responsibility does the depository institution accept?
- **Ceremony:** What does each participant do, observe, and receive?
- **Conditions:** What identity, eligibility, approval, limit, documentary, timing, or purpose rules apply?
- **Execution:** Which approved system, driver, account, and provider complete the instruction?
- **Evidence:** What must return for accounting, participant recourse, reconciliation, oversight, and audit?
- **Continuity:** What happens when data, systems, channels, providers, funds, or approvals are unavailable?
- **Readiness:** Which institution makes each decision, and on what evidence?

## Canonical Mapping

All workflow domains use the same operating progression:

```text
Authorized public purpose and external financial event
                    ↓
Funding Event and Treasury recognition
                    ↓
Lien for the authorized purpose
                    ↓
Facility for the banking service
                    ↓
Settlement Envelope for conditions and evidence
                    ↓
Pay Code or approved presentation
                    ↓
Execution through the institution-specific driver and approved provider
                    ↓
Evidence, exception resolution, and reconciliation
```

The mapping does not imply that every workflow must present a Pay Code to the end participant. A system or channel may carry the authorized instruction in another approved form while preserving the same canonical meaning and controls.

## Workflow Notes

### Government payroll

Payroll is a recurring public obligation, not merely a bulk transfer. The operating design must relate employer authority, source data, approved payroll, funding, personnel attribution, release timing, failed outcomes, correction, accounting, and evidence.

The Facility carries payroll policy and institutional responsibility. The Settlement Envelope may carry approval, employee or payee attribution, limits, and timing. Execution may be batch-oriented, but each material result must remain reconcilable.

### Pensions, statutory benefits, and veterans' benefits

Recurring benefits require continuing institutional memory. The workflow may need to govern eligibility, recurrence, identity, access, death or status events, unresolved payments, support for vulnerable recipients, and coordination among public and banking records.

Veterans' benefits form a distinct research and service context; inclusion here does not assert that a named institution administers a particular program. Institution-specific claims require the research gate in Annex F.

### LGU assistance, disaster response, and social protection

These workflows combine urgency, public purpose, participant diversity, and heightened accountability. A useful design must support local conditions without weakening authority, duplication controls, records, support, or post-program reconciliation.

The participant journey matters. A credential or voucher may simplify access, but it must remain subordinate to the Facility, Settlement Envelope, approved recipient basis, and institutional support model.

### Scholarships, grants, and agricultural support

These workflows often depend on milestones, cohorts, seasons, documents, permitted uses, or third-party institutions. The Settlement Envelope can carry those conditions while the Facility preserves the service's public and banking meaning.

The architecture should not infer legal restrictions or create purpose controls that the governing program has not established.

### Procurement and GOCC activity

Procurement settlement requires clear separation between procurement authority, contract performance, acceptance, payment approval, execution, and accounting. The platform may preserve approved evidence. It does not determine whether procurement requirements have been met.

GOCC workflows may resemble private-enterprise services in form while remaining governed by the entity's authority and public obligations. Each must be examined on its actual institutional basis.

### Collections

Collections reverse the direction of value but not the need for governance. The ceremony must relate payer, agency, obligation, account, amount, channel, receipt, posting, exceptions, correction or refund, and final reconciliation.

A payment confirmation is not sufficient when the government record cannot identify the obligation it satisfies.

### Treasury and inter-agency activity

Treasury workflows may involve recognition, reservation, scheduling, controlled movement, provider balances, account positions, and unresolved obligations. Canonical Treasury is an operational view; official treasury and accounting authority remains elsewhere.

Inter-agency activity introduces multiple authoritative records and responsibility boundaries. The design must make delegation, attribution, data sharing, settlement, evidence ownership, and consolidated reconciliation explicit.

## Cross-Cutting Control Themes

| Theme | Questions that apply to every workflow |
| --- | --- |
| Authority | Is the public and banking authority current, specific, and documented? |
| Participant data | Who is the authoritative source, and how are errors corrected? |
| Fund characterization | What is the legal, accounting, custody, and public character of the funds? |
| Accessibility | Can intended participants use the approved channels in real conditions? |
| Exceptions | Who owns failed, delayed, duplicate, reversed, unclaimed, or ambiguous outcomes? |
| Evidence | What must be retained, for how long, and by which institution? |
| Reconciliation | Which records are compared, at what frequency, and who closes differences? |
| Continuity | What alternative process exists when a channel, provider, or system is unavailable? |
| Change | What requires public-program approval, bank approval, provider certification, or a new readiness decision? |

## Workflow Selection Gate

A workflow is suitable for controlled discovery when:

1. the responsible institutions and decision owners are identified;
2. authority and material legal questions are known or assigned for resolution;
3. public purpose and banking service can be stated separately;
4. the source and character of funds and applicable accounts can be examined;
5. participant, data, evidence, and reconciliation requirements are sufficiently bounded;
6. a credible execution and provider path exists for discovery;
7. exceptions and continuity can be designed rather than ignored; and
8. the workflow is important enough to justify institutional effort and narrow enough to govern.

The Program should begin with one bounded ceremony. Expansion should reuse the governed foundation and proceed only when evidence supports the next institutional responsibility.
