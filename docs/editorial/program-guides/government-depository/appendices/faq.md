# Appendix F — Frequently Asked Questions

## What is the Program?

It is the institution-specific initiative through which an eligible government depository institution establishes a reusable governed operating Capability for selected public-finance services. It combines maintained software, institution-specific integration, controlled deployment, public and banking governance, testing, evidence, and continuing support.

## What does the Program authorize?

Nothing by itself. It does not confer government-depository authority, program eligibility, procurement preference, public funding, settlement access, or production approval. Those remain subject to applicable authority and institutional decision.

## Why is settlement connectivity not enough?

A rail or API can move funds. It does not by itself establish public purpose, validate authority, recognize and reserve resources, apply conditions, manage exceptions, support participants, preserve evidence, or reconcile the complete obligation.

## What does x-change do?

x-change coordinates governed public-finance banking ceremonies through a reusable operating grammar. It does not provide public or banking authority, appropriate funds, accept deposits, hold public money, create stored value, own institutional relationships, or become the settlement provider.

## What does `emi-core` do?

`emi-core` defines the maintained common contract for preparing and submitting approved instructions, preserving references, receiving status, handling provider responses and ambiguity, and supporting reconciliation. It does not contain the public or commercial meaning of a Facility.

## Why is an institution-specific driver necessary?

Institutional APIs differ in authentication, certificates, request formats, signatures, idempotency, webhooks, status models, limits, errors, reversals, reconciliation evidence, and versioning. `3neti/emi-{institution}` maps the common contract to one institution's approved behavior. It requires discovery, mapping, implementation, certification, and maintenance; it is not automatically available.

## What is a Pay Code?

A Pay Code is a credential representing an authorized instruction. It can be communicated, presented, validated, and executed under the governing Facility and Settlement Envelope. It is not money, a benefit, a deposit, a voucher balance, or the banking service itself.

## Can a government voucher contain a Pay Code?

Yes, when the responsible institutions approve the journey. A printed or digital voucher may carry or resolve to the credential. The voucher does not create a second Treasury, stored-value balance, or execution model.

## Where do public funds remain?

They remain within the applicable government accounts, depository accounts, and regulated settlement arrangements. Their legal, custody, appropriation, trust, and accounting character is not determined by x-change Treasury state.

## Does common software create a shared bank?

No. Each participating institution may retain its own deployment, data, credentials, release controls, provider accounts, settlement relationships, operating decisions, and audit trail. Shared software and settlement infrastructure do not require shared tenancy or shared institutional authority.

## Who owns the government relationship?

The public entity retains its program and constituent relationships. The depository institution retains its banking, depositor, customer, and government relationships. ODTI and other providers acquire neither through the Program.

## Who owns production?

The depository institution owns or controls its approved production environment and banking release decision. Production of a public workflow also depends on the approvals of the responsible public institution and other applicable authorities.

## Is NetBank or another specific settlement provider required?

No. The Program presumes no exclusive settlement institution. The depository institution uses settlement arrangements it has established and approved.

## What remains the depository institution's responsibility?

Banking authority, custody, accounts, policy, infrastructure approval, credentials, production decisions, operations, Treasury, security, risk, compliance, support, accounting, reconciliation, provider relationships, evidence, and institutional readiness remain its responsibilities within the applicable arrangement.

## What remains the government entity's responsibility?

Public purpose, program authority, funding authority, participant basis, approvals, conditions, public records, reporting, and accountability remain with the responsible public institution within its mandate.

## What does ODTI maintain?

ODTI maintains the licensed x-change software, `emi-core`, supported components, documentation, reference architecture, and testing capability within agreed scope. ODTI may perform or coordinate agreed driver work. It does not declare the institutions operationally ready.

## When is the institution ready?

Readiness exists only when the responsible institutions judge that sufficient evidence supports lawful authorization, operation, supervision, security, settlement, reconciliation, support, records, audit, and governance. Driver certification is one technical input, not the readiness decision.
