# VIS-001 Prototype Comparison

## Evaluation status

This comparison is an internal design review of the eight prototype directions. Ratings describe test hypotheses based on the canonical validation framework. They are not fabricated reader-test results.

This record describes the original exploration assets. The [Milestone 5.1 Shortlist Comparison](../milestone-5.1/SHORTLIST-COMPARISON.md) governs readiness of the corrected reader-test candidates.

No direction is approved, canonical, or selected as the production visual identity.

## Rating language

- **High** — strong expected performance requiring reader confirmation.
- **Moderate** — plausible performance with a material open question.
- **Low** — likely weakness for that criterion.
- **Pass with risk** — internal Doctrine preflight found no direct violation, but a specified viewer inference requires testing.

## Skeptical cross-direction findings

### Where does the eye go first?

- D02 and D04 lead most naturally with people.
- D03 leads with route.
- D05 leads with signs.
- D01, D06, and D08 lead with structure and credential contrast.
- D07 leads with perspective platforms.

Directions that lead with an object require testing against the principle that the human outcome remains the hero.

### What appears to move?

All directions intend the Pay Code to move. D01, D04, D06, and D08 express that distinction most literally. D02, D03, and D07 rely on paths that could be mistaken for money or system flow. D05 depends on pictographic interpretation.

### Does Claim feel like the payoff?

D02 and D07 give Claim the strongest spatial or emotional emphasis. D03, D05, and D06 make Claim structurally clear. D01 and D08 remain restrained. D04 makes the recipient active but may require close viewing.

### Is choice nested and singular?

All eight studies place Choice inside Claim and show one selected destination. D03 and D06 make the nesting most explicit. D05 makes it most distance-readable. D04 and D08 need scale testing.

## Advancement recommendation

Advance five complementary directions to first-reader testing:

1. **D03 Transit Map** — test memory strength against rail and money-flow misunderstanding.
2. **D04 Editorial Line** — test whether human narrative survives at practical sizes.
3. **D05 Pictographic Wayfinding** — test event comprehension and ticket-like interpretation.
4. **D06 Modular Card** — test semantic containment against software-UI interpretation.
5. **D08 Minimal Institutional** — test executive credibility against distance and warmth limitations.

Retain D01 as a neutral geometric control. Hold D02 as a possible human-language influence rather than a full direction. Do not advance D07 until reader evidence shows that added depth improves understanding enough to justify its complexity.

This recommendation deliberately preserves different strengths. It does not force a winner before unfamiliar readers have been observed.

## Combination hypotheses for later review

Only after testing, consider whether a combined direction could use:

- D03's memorable route;
- D04's human posture;
- D05's distance clarity;
- D06's nested Claim containment; and
- D08's institutional restraint.

Combination is not authorized during Milestone 5. Mixing untested strengths too early would create a ninth visual direction without evidence.

## Final statement

No production visual identity has been selected. Comprehension evidence must precede aesthetic convergence.
