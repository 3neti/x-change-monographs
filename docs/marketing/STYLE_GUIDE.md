# Marketing Style Guide

## Voice

Marketing language is direct, human, calm, and curious. It should sound confident enough to be remembered and restrained enough to be trusted.

Prefer:

- short active sentences;
- familiar human situations;
- one idea per paragraph;
- concrete verbs such as authorize, share, present, choose, validate, and receive;
- institutional precision in small supporting text; and
- invitations to observe or explore.

Avoid:

- startup superlatives;
- claims of disruption, revolution, inevitability, or market dominance;
- technical architecture in primary copy;
- legal qualification repeated in every panel;
- promises of universal availability, instant completion, or guaranteed outcomes;
- language suggesting that x-change is a bank, wallet, payment rail, public authority, or custodian.

## Message hierarchy

1. **Human headline** — the practical problem or possibility.
2. **Primary message** — the one-sentence Pay Code definition.
3. **Visual explanation** — Authorize, Share, Receive.
4. **Boundary caption** — the Pay Code carries the instruction, not the money.
5. **Invitation** — a next step into a demonstration or deeper publication.

If a composition cannot support all five layers, follow the compression rule in the Marketing Grammar.

## Naming and capitalization

- Write `x-change` in lowercase with the hyphen intact and keep the wordmark on one line.
- Write `Pay Code` as two words with initial capitals.
- Write `Pay Codes` for the plural.
- Use `recipient`, `sender`, `responsible institution`, and `participating financial provider` consistently.
- Do not introduce product-style variants such as `PayCode`, `paycode`, or `X-Change`.

## Typography

- Use an elegant serif or humanist face for the principal message where the format permits.
- Use a restrained sans serif for labels, steps, navigation, and calls to action.
- Keep headlines compact and avoid oversized display treatment that overwhelms the illustration.
- Use comfortable line length and generous whitespace.
- Do not use all caps for the x-change wordmark.

Typography is a renderer concern. Canonical Markdown specifies hierarchy, not point sizes.

## Color

Use the established x-change publication palette as the base:

- dark institutional ink;
- warm paper or cream;
- neutral rules and panels; and
- the approved x-change orange logo or accent asset.

Do not derive a new brand palette in a marketing manuscript. Renderers should reference governed publication styles and approved brand assets. Orange is an accent, not a substitute for hierarchy or whitespace.

## Illustration-to-copy ratio

Aim for approximately 80% visual explanation and 20% copy. This is a communication target rather than a mechanical area calculation. A reader who ignores the paragraphs should still understand the basic journey.

## Calls to action

Use one primary invitation per composition. Prefer `See`, `Explore`, `Bring`, `Discuss`, or `Read`. Avoid `Buy now`, `Sign up`, `Deploy`, `Go live`, or language that presumes an institutional decision.

## Required restraint

Long legal disclaimers do not belong in the headline path. When a boundary is material, state it once in plain language:

> The Pay Code carries the instruction—not the money. Availability and completion depend on supported institutions, channels, and conditions.

Deeper legal, regulatory, custody, settlement, and implementation treatment belongs in the relevant Program Guide, Proposal, or agreement.

## Format adaptation

The same source message may be shortened, rearranged, or revealed progressively by a renderer only when the canonical wording variants are already governed in the Marketing Grammar. A renderer must not invent a new claim to make a layout fit.
