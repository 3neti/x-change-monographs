# Marketing Spatial Grammar

## Purpose

Spatial Grammar defines what visual relationships mean in the x-change Marketing Design System. It governs relative position, orientation, depth, grouping, and direction without prescribing coordinates, page geometry, or renderer behavior.

Motion explains change. Spatial Grammar explains relationship.

## Human focus

People occupy the visual foreground. Technology and infrastructure remain behind, beneath, or between the human actions they support.

The human outcome receives the strongest destination emphasis. The Pay Code may be visually distinctive for continuity, but it must not displace the sender's intention or recipient's agency as the subject of the composition.

## Canonical sides

Within one reading direction:

- Generate begins on the sender or responsible-institution side.
- Share occupies the connective middle.
- Claim concludes on the recipient side.
- Evidence may return toward the responsible context using a subordinate trace.

The actual physical direction may be left-to-right, right-to-left for an approved language context, or top-to-bottom. Semantic origin, connection, and destination must remain clear.

## Orientation of people

- The sender faces the intended journey or recipient.
- The recipient faces the arriving Pay Code or selected supported destination.
- During Claim, the recipient's orientation shows active presentation and choice.
- People do not face away from the action merely to satisfy symmetry.

## Pay Code position

The Pay Code occupies different relationships at different moments:

- within the governed frame during Generate;
- between sender and recipient during Share;
- with the recipient at the beginning of Claim; and
- before, not inside, the selected destination during completion.

The Pay Code must not sit inside a cash container, account balance, wallet, settlement resource, or human body.

At every position, the primary object remains a readable alphanumeric credential framed by parallel-line marks. A QR companion may sit beside the credential or among Share channel cues only when smaller and spatially subordinate. The QR may not replace the credential, lead the hierarchy, or imply required scanning.

## Institutional depth

Institutions provide structural context. They remain visually behind people unless the composition specifically answers an institutional question.

Use enclosing frames, counters, gateways, or bounded fields to show responsibility. Do not enlarge institutional architecture until it becomes the hero of a human-first composition.

When VIS-002 is the subject, separate institutions may become prominent, but the operating layer remains subordinate to their authority and roles.

## Choice geometry

Choice exists inside Claim.

- Branches emerge only after the recipient presents the Pay Code.
- Branches move downward or forward from the Claim path rather than backward toward Generate.
- The set is visibly bounded.
- Destinations carry equal neutral weight until one is selected.
- Unselected paths recede.
- The selected path rejoins one completion.

Choice may not occupy the same visual level as Generate, Share, and Claim.

## Journey geometry

The primary Journey is a single continuous path through three moments.

- Solid direction indicates forward progression.
- Spatial gaps indicate meaningful pauses, not broken responsibility.
- The credential maintains continuity across gaps.
- The path may bend to suit a surface but may not loop, fork before Claim, or reverse.

Avoid hub-and-spoke diagrams for VIS-001. They imply platform centrality rather than a human journey.

## Communication position

Communication lies between sender and recipient. Channel cues remain adjacent to the path and subordinate to the Pay Code.

A phone, QR, email, message, voice, or printed paper cue may identify how the credential travels. It must remain subordinate to the human-readable alphanumeric Pay Code, must not appear beneath the institutional settlement layer, and must not suggest that the channel moves money.

## Validation and settlement position

Validation sits after presentation and before completion. Settlement sits behind the selected institutional path.

Both remain spatially inside Claim or an institutional supporting composition. Neither should interrupt the public path as a separate station.

## Evidence position

Evidence follows completion and returns toward the responsible context.

- Use a dotted, thinner, or otherwise secondary relationship.
- Keep the forward journey dominant.
- Do not place Evidence ahead of completion.
- Do not let the return trace resemble reversed funds.

## Unknown destination

In ILL-001 and VIS-005, uncertainty belongs beside the destination cluster. The known recipient remains clear and unambiguous.

A question mark, faded options, or unresolved branch may indicate destination uncertainty. Never place the uncertainty marker over the person.

## Application groups

VIS-003 and VIS-004 use repeated spatial grammar:

```text
responsible source → Pay Code → intended person
```

Tiles should share direction, Pay Code position, and recipient emphasis. Purpose cues distinguish applications; layout novelty does not.

## Proximity rules

Elements placed close together imply one governed relationship:

- Approval stays close to Instruction.
- Pay Code stays close to the sender during Generate and the recipient during Claim.
- Choice stays close to the recipient and selected Destination.
- Validation stays close to the selected path.
- Evidence stays close to Completion before returning.
- The x-change operating layer stays close to the governed instruction, not to funds or custody.

Whitespace separates different moments and institutional roles. It must not sever causal continuity.

## Scale rules

Relative scale communicates importance:

1. Human intention and outcome.
2. The three journey moments.
3. Pay Code continuity.
4. Supported destination and institutional role.
5. Validation, settlement, evidence, and other nested detail.

Large credentials, QR codes, bank façades, or infrastructure may be visually striking but invert the canonical hierarchy.

## Layering rules

Use three semantic planes where depth is helpful:

```text
Foreground: human action and outcome
Middle:     Pay Code and governed journey
Background: institutions, infrastructure, conditions, evidence
```

These are relationships, not visual effects. Flat artwork may express the same hierarchy through weight, grouping, and whitespace.

## Spatial tests

### Focus Test

Does the eye reach the person and intended outcome before infrastructure?

### Direction Test

Can the viewer follow Generate → Share → Claim without reading labels?

### Nesting Test

Is Choice unmistakably inside Claim?

### Singular-Outcome Test

Do all visible alternatives resolve into one selected path and one completion?

### Responsibility Test

Are institutional boundaries visible without implying shared authority or x-change custody?

### Evidence Test

Is the return trace clearly subordinate and distinct from financial movement?

## Renderer independence

Spatial relationships survive changes in medium, orientation, dimensions, typography, and illustration style. A renderer chooses geometry. It does not choose meaning.
