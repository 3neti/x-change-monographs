# Institution Intelligence Framework

## Status

Established — Phase 1, Version 1.0

## Purpose

The Institution Intelligence Framework governs how canonical x-change banking doctrine is applied to a specific financial institution.

An Institution Edition must never begin with writing. It begins with disciplined institutional understanding based on attributable evidence.

The framework answers one governing question:

> How does this institution relate to the existing canonical doctrine?

It does not authorize new doctrine, architecture, operating grammar, legal characterization, pricing, or implementation commitments.

## Canonical lineage

Institution work belongs to a controlled lineage:

```text
Doctrine
   ↓
Institution Intelligence
   ↓
Strategic Gap Analysis
   ↓
Editorial Mapping
   ↓
Institution Edition
   ↓
Commercial Proposal
   ↓
Implementation Roadmap
```

The layers are cumulative but not interchangeable. A later artifact may rely on an earlier artifact; it does not replace it.

## Artifact architecture

| Artifact | Governing question | Function | Must not become |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Canonical doctrine | What remains true across the sector or Program? | Establishes authoritative concepts, boundaries, and operating grammar | Institution-specific interpretation |
| Institution Intelligence | What kind of institution is this, based on evidence? | Records observed institutional facts, bounded inferences, and source limitations | A sales narrative or proposal |
| Strategic Gap Analysis | Where does current evidence align with, differ from, or leave questions about canonical doctrine? | Identifies alignment, gaps, unknowns, and possible areas for further evaluation | A judgment that the institution is deficient |
| Editorial Mapping | How should the canonical guide be adapted for this institution? | Produces the approved writing brief, claim map, narrative emphasis, and example selection | A manuscript |
| Institution Edition | How does the canonical Program apply to this institution? | Adapts examples, priorities, language, ceremonies, and sequence without changing doctrine | New architecture or a commercial offer |
| Commercial Proposal | What institution-specific work is being proposed? | Defines proposed scope, services, responsibilities, assumptions, and commercial structure | Evidence about the institution or an implementation plan |
| Implementation Roadmap | How could approved work progress responsibly? | Defines discovery, driver work, implementation, pilot, readiness, and expansion gates | A production authorization or guaranteed schedule |

## Required gates

Work may advance only when the preceding artifact is sufficiently complete for its purpose.

### Gate 1 — Evidence readiness

Institution Intelligence may enter review only when:

- material claims have attributable sources;
- reporting periods and access dates are recorded;
- observed, inferred, and recommended statements are distinguishable;
- material contradictions and unknowns are recorded; and
- public-source limitations are disclosed.

### Gate 2 — Analytical readiness

Strategic Gap Analysis may begin only from reviewed Institution Intelligence. It must cite the evidence it uses and the canonical doctrine against which it compares that evidence.

### Gate 3 — Editorial readiness

Editorial Mapping may begin only after the principal evidence and gap findings have been reviewed. Unresolved questions must remain visible; they must not be repaired through confident prose.

### Gate 4 — Writing readiness

An Institution Edition may be drafted only from an accepted Editorial Mapping. The mapping must identify the governing canonical source, permitted adaptations, protected doctrine, evidence-backed claims, selected ceremonies, and unresolved matters.

### Gate 5 — Proposal readiness

A Commercial Proposal may be prepared only after the Institution Edition has established the institution-specific narrative and scope context. A proposal may add proposed terms; it may not retroactively alter research findings or doctrine.

### Gate 6 — Implementation readiness

An Implementation Roadmap may become institution-specific only after the relevant proposal, authorities, responsibilities, dependencies, and discovery requirements are understood. Roadmap completion is not production readiness.

## Evidence classes

Every material institution-specific statement must be identifiable as one of the following:

- **Observed** — directly supported by an identified source.
- **Inferred** — a bounded interpretation drawn from identified observations.
- **Recommended** — a proposed action, emphasis, or application of doctrine.
- **Unknown** — material information not established by available evidence.
- **Institution-Provided** — information intentionally supplied by the institution and governed by its stated access and use conditions.

Observed evidence does not automatically make an inference correct. A recommendation does not become an institutional intention until the institution adopts it.

## Doctrine boundary

Institution-specific work may adapt:

- audience language;
- institutional examples;
- product and customer emphasis;
- representative banking ceremonies;
- implementation priorities;
- rollout sequence;
- governance emphasis; and
- institution-specific responsibilities and dependencies supported by evidence or approved discovery.

It may not casually redefine:

- Pay Code;
- Facility;
- Settlement Envelope;
- Treasury;
- Funding Event;
- Execution Engine;
- Execution Driver;
- custody and authority boundaries;
- software ownership;
- settlement responsibility;
- readiness as an institutional judgment; or
- other frozen doctrine identified by the governing Program Guide or Editorial Decision Register.

If institution evidence appears to require doctrinal change, the work must stop at the point of conflict and identify whether an Editorial Decision Record is required.

## Agent balk rule

Codex or another contributor must not conceal missing evidence, incomplete lineage, unresolved doctrinal conflict, or a skipped artifact by producing polished prose.

The report must identify:

1. the missing or conflicting requirement;
2. the affected artifact and decision gate;
3. why the information matters;
4. whether the matter is unknown, intentionally deferred, or potentially doctrinal;
5. the recommended research or governance action; and
6. whether work may safely continue without resolution.

Use language such as:

> Institution Intelligence is not yet sufficient for editorial mapping. The available public evidence does not establish the institution's settlement operating model or current payroll priorities. These matters should remain Unknown pending additional research or institution-provided discovery; they must not be inferred from product availability alone.

Missing evidence must not be manufactured to satisfy a template.

## Governance and change control

Institution Intelligence, Strategic Gap Analysis, Editorial Mapping, Institution Editions, proposals, and roadmaps are living artifacts. Their dates, sources, review states, and superseding versions must remain visible.

Canonical doctrine remains frozen unless reopened through an accepted Editorial Decision Record. Field experience may reveal a possible doctrinal improvement, but institution-specific work cannot adopt that improvement as doctrine before governance approval.

The template under `docs/institutions/template/` is the required starting scaffold. A future institution repository may add supporting files, but it must preserve the artifact boundaries and gates established here.
