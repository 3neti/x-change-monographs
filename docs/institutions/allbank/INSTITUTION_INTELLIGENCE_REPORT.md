## Executive Summary

**Observed.** AllBank is a long-established Philippine thrift bank. Its audited corporate history traces registration to 1961 and commercial operations to 1963. It operated previously as Optimum Development Bank, adopted the AllBank name through 2017 approvals, and completed a merger with Rural Bank of Plaridel in 2019. PDIC listed it as an insured thrift bank headquartered in Mandaluyong as of October 2025. [CLM-001–CLM-005]

**Observed.** The institution publicly positions itself around working-class Filipinos, business owners, corporations, MSMEs, and entrepreneurs. Its disclosed portfolio spans deposits, salary, auto, housing and commercial lending, payroll, corporate banking, merchant payments, collections, remittance, QR, mobile banking, and online account opening. [CLM-006, CLM-008–CLM-016, CLM-029–CLM-031]

**Observed.** BSP reported ₱9.903 billion in total assets, ₱7.366 billion in deposits, and ₱2.679 billion in net loans and receivables at 31 March 2026. BSP’s year-end 2025 table reported 29.14% ROE. These measures describe different reporting dates and should not be combined into a single-period performance conclusion. [CLM-017–CLM-020]

**Inferred.** The public record presents AllBank as a hybrid physical-digital institution with visible strengths in payment-network participation, corporate transaction controls, QR merchant services, and partnership-led distribution. Payroll, merchant payments, collections, and remittance are credible candidates for later editorial evaluation. Public evidence does not establish which of them management considers strategically primary. [INF-001–INF-003, INF-006–INF-007]

**Unknown.** Current beneficial ownership, current branch and ATM counts, active channel usage, customer composition, product economics, internal technology architecture, API readiness, settlement-account design, service-level evidence, and management’s present priority order were not established by the reviewed public sources. These gaps must be resolved through management discovery before an Institution Edition can be authorized. [UNK-001–UNK-016]

## Method and Source Base

This report applies the canonical Institution Intelligence Framework and Public Source Policy. It uses 25 public sources: official annual reports and audited statements, BSP statistical and payment-participant records, a PDIC directory, live AllBank channel pages and forms, official app-market listings, identified partner announcements, reputable reporting, and a public company profile.

Material statements are marked **Observed**, **Inferred**, **Recommended**, or **Unknown** and traced to claim IDs in [EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md](EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md). Source records appear in [PUBLIC_SOURCE_REGISTER.md](PUBLIC_SOURCE_REGISTER.md).

The most recent detailed official annual report located covers 2023. Current selected financial measures and payment-participant status are available through BSP records for 2025 and the first quarter of 2026. Product, branch, and partnership details from older reports are presented with their dates; they are not silently treated as current.

No institution-provided material was used. No claim is made about AllBank’s confidential systems, commercial intentions, regulatory standing beyond cited public records, or willingness to adopt x-change.

## Institutional Profile

### History and institutional development

**Observed.** AllBank’s 2020 audited notes state that the bank was registered with the SEC as a domestic thrift bank on 31 October 1961 and authorized by the BSP to begin commercial operations on 7 January 1963. The same notes record the 2017 change from Optimum Development Bank, Inc. to AllBank (A Thrift Bank), Inc. and the 2019 completion of a merger in which AllBank survived and absorbed Rural Bank of Plaridel, Inc. [CLM-001–CLM-004]

### Ownership and governance context

**Observed.** The reviewed official sources identify AllBank as a domestic thrift bank and provide its corporate office, directors, officers, and governance disclosures for particular reporting years. [SRC-001, SRC-002, SRC-004, SRC-005]

**Unknown.** The reviewed authoritative public sources did not establish the current legal and beneficial ownership structure. Public associations, common locations, or third-party descriptions are insufficient substitutes for an authoritative ownership disclosure. [UNK-001]

### Regulatory and institutional classification

**Observed.** PDIC’s October 2025 directory lists AllBank among insured thrift banks. BSP’s March 2026 tables include it in the thrift-bank group. [CLM-005, CLM-017–CLM-020]

### Markets and customers

**Observed.** AllBank’s 2023 report describes products and services for working-class Filipinos, business owners, corporations, MSMEs, and entrepreneurs. The public portfolio includes household deposit and lending services as well as payroll, merchant, collection, and corporate transaction services. [CLM-006, CLM-008–CLM-010]

**Unknown.** Public descriptions do not establish the number, distribution, profitability, or priority of those customer segments. [UNK-004, UNK-011]

### Geographic presence

**Observed.** The 2023 annual report names 16 branches across Mandaluyong, Las Piñas, Taguig, Cavite, Laguna, Bulacan, Bataan, and Pampanga. A 2020 audited source reported 17 branches and 29 ATMs. [CLM-007, SRC-004]

**Unknown.** A current authoritative branch, ATM, POS cash-out, and merchant count was not located. The dated figures are therefore not reconciled into a present count. [UNK-002]

### Business model

**Observed.** AllBank describes a model that combines deposit taking, consumer and business financing, electronic banking, corporate transaction services, merchant payments, and partner-enabled distribution. [CLM-006, CLM-008–CLM-016]

**Inferred.** Its public proposition is broader than a single lending product or single digital channel. It connects household banking, employer and employee relationships, business cash management, payments, and merchant acceptance. The relative economic contribution of each component remains Unknown. [INF-002, UNK-012]

## Products and Services

### Consumer and deposits

**Observed.** The 2023 product list includes regular, ATM, children’s, teen, flexible, and Quick Saver accounts; checking products; payroll accounts; peso time deposits; and foreign-currency savings and time deposits. [CLM-008]

**Observed.** Consumer lending products listed in 2023 include salary, auto, and housing loans. [CLM-009]

**Unknown.** The current availability, balances, underwriting priorities, channel eligibility, and uptake of each product are not established. [UNK-005, UNK-016]

### SME and corporate banking

**Observed.** AllBank publicly lists commercial loans, corporate internet banking, bulk sending, payroll, account sweeping, bills payment, deposit pick-up, night depository, and PDC warehousing. AllConnect materials identify corporate account holders and maker/authorizer controls. [CLM-010, CLM-013–CLM-014, CLM-030–CLM-031]

**Inferred.** These services indicate a meaningful public proposition for enterprises and employers, but they do not establish market share, utilization, or management priority. [INF-002]

### Housing

**Observed.** The 2023 report lists a housing loan for purchase, construction, renovation, refinancing, or investment. [CLM-009]

**Unknown.** Portfolio size, developer relationships, documentary workflow, disbursement stages, and digital servicing are not public in the reviewed evidence. [UNK-005, UNK-009, UNK-010]

### Merchant, payments, and collections

**Observed.** Public services include POS debit acquiring, POS cash-out, QRPh P2M, AllPay Bills, bills payment, PDC warehousing, corporate bulk sending, deposit pick-up, and night depository. AllBank also exposes a P2M merchant portal. [CLM-010, CLM-015, CLM-031]

**Observed.** BSP lists AllBank as sender/receiver in InstaPay, PESONet, QR Ph P2P, QR Ph P2M, and Bills Pay PH as of 31 March 2026. [CLM-016]

**Unknown.** Merchant counts, transaction volumes, settlement terms, service levels, exception patterns, reconciliation design, and revenue are not public. [UNK-008–UNK-012]

### Payroll

**Observed.** AllBank’s public product list includes payroll accounts and payroll services. The AllConnect enrollment form includes payroll among corporate functions. [CLM-010, CLM-030]

**Unknown.** Employer count, employee count, payroll funding method, exception handling, lending cross-sell, and strategic importance are not established. [UNK-004, UNK-009–UNK-011]

### Remittance

**Observed.** The 2023 annual report lists Western Union remittance services delivered with identified partners. [CLM-029]

**Unknown.** Current availability, corridors, customer journey, funding model, recipient controls, volumes, and economics were not established. [UNK-005, UNK-009, UNK-016]

### Treasury and government services

**Observed.** Public product materials identify foreign-currency deposit and foreign-exchange services. [SRC-001]

**Unknown.** The reviewed sources do not establish a broader public Treasury proposition, operational Treasury practices relevant to proposed ceremonies, or material government disbursement and collection services. [UNK-013, UNK-014]

## Digital Banking Profile

### Current channels

**Observed.** Publicly visible channels include branches, iOS and Android mobile apps, AllConnect institutional internet banking, online e-KYC, a P2M merchant portal, ATM/BancNet access, POS cash-out and acquiring, and national retail-payment rails. [CLM-007, CLM-011–CLM-016]

**Inferred.** AllBank is publicly visible as multichannel rather than digital-only. The bank appears to use digital channels to extend established banking and partner services. [INF-001, INF-007]

### Customer journeys

**Observed.** The online e-KYC surface presents digital account-opening options. AllConnect presents corporate access and configurable transaction functions. Mobile and QR channels support account access, transfer, and payment journeys described in AllBank’s annual reports. [CLM-011–CLM-016]

**Unknown.** Completion rates, turnaround times, assisted handoffs, abandonment, accessibility, customer satisfaction, exception resolution, and channel-to-branch continuity are not public. [UNK-009, UNK-010]

### Digital initiatives and partnerships

**Observed.** AllBank’s 2023 report describes digital-payment partnerships with UMI Technologies, Dragonpay, PearlPay, JustPayTo, and Salmon. SAFC announced a QRPh collaboration in 2024. A March 2026 partner feature reported a Manny Pay collaboration. [CLM-022–CLM-025]

**Inferred.** Partnership-led QR and payment distribution is a recurring pattern in the public record. The sources do not establish whether these arrangements share a reusable internal operating model. [INF-003]

## Strategic Direction

**Observed.** AllBank’s 2023 report states an intention to widen financial products and electronic services and to serve corporations, MSMEs, entrepreneurs, and working-class Filipinos through digital and physical channels. It highlights QR and partner distribution. [CLM-006, CLM-022–CLM-023]

**Observed.** Subsequent public sources show continued QR/payment partnership activity in 2024 and 2026 and active BSP participation across major retail-payment systems in March 2026. [CLM-016, CLM-024–CLM-025]

**Inferred.** The strongest public strategic pattern is not a single app feature but the extension of banking and payment services through corporate controls, merchants, employers, and external partners. [INF-002–INF-004]

**Unknown.** Management’s current order of priorities across deposit growth, lending, housing, payroll, merchants, collections, partnerships, and operational modernization remains undisclosed. [UNK-011]

## Opportunities for Canonical Alignment

**Recommended.** Evaluate payroll, merchant payments, collections, and remittance during management discovery before choosing the dominant ceremony for an AllBank Institution Edition. Each is evidenced publicly, but none is established as management’s preferred starting responsibility. [REC-001]

**Recommended.** Preserve remittance as the canonical explanatory default unless reviewed institution evidence supports a more representative lead ceremony. [REC-002]

**Recommended.** Treat current payment-system participation as an institutional asset while requiring separate discovery of settlement authority, operating controls, APIs, evidence, and driver feasibility. [REC-003]

**Recommended.** If management confirms their strategic importance, give corporate controls, partner distribution, and recurring employer and merchant relationships greater editorial attention. [REC-004]
