# Commercial Proposal Standard

## Purpose

A Commercial Proposal defines the institution-specific work proposed after the Institution Edition has established the institutional context.

The proposal is not Institution Intelligence, canonical doctrine, an Institution Edition, a legal agreement, or an implementation authorization.

## Required inputs

- accepted Institution Edition or approved edition-stage brief;
- governing canonical doctrine and Program Guide;
- known institution priorities and decision context;
- proposal-specific discovery or institution-provided information;
- identified responsibilities and dependencies; and
- approved commercial and legal authority for the proposal stage.

## Required proposal coverage

As relevant, describe:

- proposed objective and scope;
- institution and provider responsibilities;
- discovery;
- institution-specific driver discovery and development;
- professional services;
- deployment;
- integrations;
- training;
- pilot;
- support and maintenance;
- dependencies and assumptions;
- exclusions;
- acceptance or governance gates;
- proposed commercial structure; and
- next decisions.

No commercial numbers are required by this standard. When numbers are later introduced, they must identify assumptions, units, taxes, validity periods, dependencies, and approval status.

## Driver language

Do not describe an institution-specific Execution Driver as automatically available.

Where required, the proposal should distinguish:

- institutional discovery;
- API and settlement-interface mapping;
- implementation;
- testing and certification;
- deployment integration; and
- continuing maintenance.

Driver certification does not by itself establish institutional or operational readiness.

## Fact and proposal boundary

The proposal must distinguish:

- **Established context** — traceable to accepted intelligence or institution-provided material;
- **Proposed scope** — work offered for consideration;
- **Assumption** — a condition used for planning but not yet verified;
- **Dependency** — an external decision, capability, access, authority, or service required;
- **Exclusion** — work or responsibility outside scope; and
- **Term** — a commercial or legal condition subject to approval.

Proposed terms must not be written back into Institution Intelligence as facts.

## Doctrine and architecture

A proposal may configure and apply the canonical framework. It may not redefine doctrine to make a proposed scope appear complete.

If discovery reveals a possible doctrinal conflict, record it separately and invoke editorial governance. Commercial urgency does not authorize doctrinal change.

## Relationship to implementation

A signed or accepted proposal may authorize defined work under the applicable agreements. It does not automatically place a service into production.

Implementation, pilot, operational readiness, and expansion remain governed institutional activities.
