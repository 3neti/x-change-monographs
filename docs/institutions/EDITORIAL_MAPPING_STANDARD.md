# Editorial Mapping Standard

## Purpose

Editorial Mapping is the bridge between research and writing.

It determines how an approved canonical Program Guide should adapt to a specific institution. Its output is a writing brief, not a manuscript.

## Required inputs

- Institution Intelligence accepted for analysis;
- reviewed Strategic Gap Analysis;
- governing Institutional Study or sector doctrine;
- applicable Program Guide and edition architecture;
- relevant Editorial Decision Records; and
- the intended institution-edition audience and use.

## Governing questions

Editorial Mapping should resolve, where evidence permits:

- Should remittance remain the explanatory ceremony?
- Should payroll become dominant?
- Should collections receive greater emphasis?
- Should merchant services become central?
- Should housing or SME workflows become central?
- Which canonical examples remain useful?
- Which examples should be replaced by institution-relevant examples?
- Should implementation sequencing change?
- Should governance, relationship, deposit, lending, public-service, or operating-sovereignty emphasis change?
- Which matters remain unknown and must not appear as facts?

These are editorial decisions within canonical doctrine, not invitations to redesign the doctrine.

## Required writing brief

### Edition Identity

Record:

- institution name;
- canonical parent guide and edition;
- governing doctrinal source;
- intended audience;
- intended decision or reading purpose;
- proposed edition identity; and
- evidence cutoff inherited from Institution Intelligence.

### Institutional Thesis

State in a short paragraph how the institution relates to the canonical Program. The thesis must be supported by the approved analysis and must not introduce new doctrine.

### Protected Doctrine

List the propositions, boundaries, terminology, and architecture that the edition may not change.

### Permitted Adaptations

List approved changes to examples, emphasis, ceremony choice, terminology, sequence, customer journeys, governance emphasis, and implementation priorities.

### Audience Questions

Identify the principal questions the edition must answer for Board, executive, operating, technology, risk, treasury, or partner readers as applicable.

### Narrative Progression

Map the institution-specific narrative to the canonical guide architecture. For each canonical section, record:

| Canonical section | Institution-specific question | Evidence-backed emphasis | Examples or ceremonies | Protected concepts | Unknowns or exclusions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

The mapping may change emphasis. It may not silently omit a mandatory canonical business question.

### Ceremony Selection

For every proposed representative ceremony, record:

- why it is institutionally relevant;
- supporting evidence;
- customer and institutional participants;
- canonical concepts it illustrates;
- why it belongs in the narrative; and
- unresolved operational assumptions.

The canonical remittance ceremony may remain, recede, or be supplemented only when the mapping explains the editorial reason and preserves doctrinal clarity.

### Claim and Evidence Map

Record every material institution-specific claim expected in the edition:

| Proposed claim | Classification | Evidence IDs | Permitted wording | Qualification | Planned location |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Recommendations must not be worded as current institutional facts.

### Terminology Map

Identify institution-preferred public terminology and its relationship to canonical terms. Institutional language may aid readability; it must not redefine canonical grammar.

### Inclusion and Exclusion Register

State what the edition will address and what remains outside scope. Explicit exclusions prevent unresolved research from reappearing as casual prose.

### Visual and Evidence Brief

Identify existing canonical diagrams that remain applicable, institution-specific figures that may be needed, and the evidence required for any new figure. Do not create production diagrams in the mapping artifact.

### Open Questions

List unresolved factual, doctrinal, legal, commercial, or implementation questions. Assign each to research, institution discovery, editorial governance, proposal development, or implementation discovery.

### Drafting Authorization

Conclude with one of these statuses:

- **Not Ready for Drafting**
- **Ready for Limited Drafting** — identified sections may proceed while recorded questions remain isolated.
- **Ready for Drafting**
- **Deviation Decision Required**

## Canonical parity rule

Before authorizing drafting, compare the proposed mapping with the canonical Program Guide architecture and mandatory coverage register.

If a required business question is absent, report the omission. Do not compensate with institution-specific prose. A structural or doctrinal deviation requires the applicable editorial governance process.

## Acceptance criteria

Editorial Mapping may authorize drafting only when:

- canonical lineage is explicit;
- the institutional thesis is evidence-based;
- protected doctrine and permitted adaptations are listed;
- every canonical section has a mapping or governed deviation;
- selected ceremonies have an editorial and evidentiary basis;
- material claims have approved wording and sources;
- unknowns and exclusions are visible; and
- no commercial proposal or implementation commitment has been smuggled into the writing brief.
