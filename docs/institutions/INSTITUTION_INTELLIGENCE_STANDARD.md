# Institution Intelligence Standard

## Purpose

Institution Intelligence is the evidence-based institutional report that precedes analysis and writing.

It establishes what is publicly known, what may reasonably be inferred, what remains unknown, and what institution-specific opportunities may merit later evaluation. It is not an Institution Edition, sales document, due-diligence opinion, legal assessment, or commercial proposal.

## Required front matter

Record:

- institution name;
- institution type and applicable canonical lineage;
- report version and status;
- evidence cutoff date;
- date last verified;
- research scope;
- public-source basis;
- institution-provided materials, if any; and
- material limitations.

## Required report structure

### Executive Summary

Answer: **What kind of institution is this?**

Summarize institutional identity, markets, business model, strategic direction, material strengths, material constraints, and principal areas for later evaluation. Draft this section last. Do not introduce facts or recommendations not supported in the report.

### Method and Source Base

State the research period, source classes used, evidence cutoff, classifications, limitations, conflicts, and any calculations.

### Institutional Profile

Cover, where evidenced:

- history and institutional development;
- ownership and group relationships;
- regulatory and institutional classification;
- markets and customer segments served;
- geographic presence and branches;
- business model; and
- publicly stated institutional purpose or positioning.

Distinguish the institution from its parent, subsidiaries, affiliates, partners, and sector associations.

### Products and Services

Assess only evidenced offerings and organize them as relevant:

- consumer;
- SME;
- corporate;
- housing;
- treasury;
- cards;
- digital banking;
- merchant services;
- payroll;
- collections;
- government services;
- remittance; and
- other public services.

Record whether an offering is current, announced, historical, partner-delivered, or uncertain. Product availability does not by itself establish adoption, profitability, technical architecture, or strategic priority.

### Financial Overview

Use public information only unless institution-provided information is separately identified.

Potential measures include:

- assets;
- deposits;
- loans;
- capital;
- ROE;
- ROA;
- NPL;
- CASA;
- revenue mix; and
- growth.

For every material figure, state the period, currency and unit, reporting perimeter, source, and relevant definition. Tables should separate disclosed figures from calculated figures.

Do not invent missing values or compare incompatible periods without qualification.

### Digital Banking Profile

Cover publicly evidenced current channels and customer experiences, including where relevant:

- internet banking;
- mobile banking;
- cards;
- merchant services;
- QR;
- collections;
- payroll;
- customer journeys;
- digital initiatives; and
- publicly announced partnerships or channel changes.

Describe what the evidence shows the customer can do. Do not infer undisclosed operating architecture from the visible channel.

### Technology Signals

Record only publicly evidenced signals such as:

- technology partnerships;
- public hiring;
- disclosed engineering capability;
- cloud references;
- security certifications or programs;
- API references; and
- named technology vendors.

For each signal, state what it supports and what it does not establish. Never assume that an institution-specific Execution Driver exists or is ready.

### Strategic Direction

Summarize management's apparent direction from public evidence. Possible themes include retail growth, SME banking, consumer banking, housing, digital transformation, regional expansion, efficiency, partnerships, and deposit growth.

Use **Observed** for explicit priorities and **Inferred** for patterns drawn across evidence. Do not assign an intention merely because a capability would be useful.

### Institutional Strengths

Identify evidence-based strengths relevant to the institution's role, relationships, capabilities, market position, or execution capacity. Avoid generic praise.

### Institutional Constraints

Identify evidence-based limitations, dependencies, pressures, or unanswered questions. A constraint is not necessarily a failing. Distinguish public evidence from analytical interpretation.

### Opportunities for Canonical Alignment

Identify possible areas where canonical doctrine may become relevant. Label these statements **Recommended** and connect each recommendation to evidence.

Do not select final ceremonies, implementation scope, vendors, pricing, or architecture in this section. Those decisions belong to later artifacts.

### Unknowns and Research Limits

List material matters not established by available evidence. Include questions that may require institution-provided discovery.

### Sources

Provide the source register or a stable reference to it. Every material factual claim must be traceable.

## Writing standard

The report should be:

- factual before interpretive;
- concise but sufficiently evidenced;
- explicit about dates and scope;
- restrained in tone;
- free of marketing language; and
- readable by executive, editorial, technology, operations, risk, and commercial reviewers.

Use tables for repeated exact mappings, financial series, product inventories, and evidence classifications. Use prose for institutional interpretation.

## Acceptance criteria

Institution Intelligence is **Accepted for Analysis** only when:

- all required sections are present or explicitly marked Unknown;
- material observations have sources;
- inferences name their evidentiary basis;
- recommendations remain distinct from facts;
- financial figures are period- and unit-specific;
- technology claims remain within public evidence;
- contradictions and limitations are recorded; and
- the Executive Summary accurately represents the completed report.
