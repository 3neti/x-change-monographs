# Public Source Policy

## Purpose

Institution Intelligence uses publicly available information unless additional material is intentionally provided by the institution for an authorized purpose.

This policy protects evidence quality, attribution, institutional fairness, and the boundary between research and speculation.

## Permitted public sources

Relevant sources may include:

- annual reports and audited financial statements;
- BSP disclosures and regulatory publications;
- SEC filings and other official registries;
- official presentations and investor materials;
- the institution's official website;
- public speeches and interviews by authorized institutional representatives;
- press releases and sustainability reports;
- public digital-banking, product, service, and support pages;
- app-store descriptions and release notes;
- public job postings;
- public procurement or partnership notices;
- reputable news reporting; and
- other lawfully accessible public records relevant to the research question.

Availability alone does not establish reliability. Sources must be evaluated for authority, date, purpose, completeness, and proximity to the claim.

## Source preference

Prefer sources in this order when they address the same claim:

1. regulator, official registry, or audited disclosure;
2. institution-issued report, filing, policy, product page, or announcement;
3. named statement by an authorized institutional representative;
4. primary documentation from an identified partner or provider;
5. reputable independent reporting; and
6. secondary commentary used only with appropriate caution.

A weaker source may identify a research lead. It should not displace a stronger source that directly establishes the matter.

## Required source record

Every source used materially must record:

- source ID;
- title;
- publisher or issuing institution;
- document or publication date, where available;
- URL or stable repository reference;
- date accessed;
- reporting period, where applicable;
- source type;
- relevant pages or sections;
- claims supported;
- any material limitation; and
- whether a local evidence copy is permitted and retained.

Links alone are not sufficient research notes. The source register must explain what the source establishes.

## Statement classification

### Observed

An observed statement is directly supported by an identified source.

Use factual language and cite the source near the claim. Preserve the source's reporting period, scope, units, qualifications, and institutional terminology.

### Inferred

An inferred statement is a bounded interpretation of one or more observations.

It must:

- identify the observations on which it depends;
- use appropriately qualified language;
- identify credible alternative explanations when material; and
- never be presented as a confirmed institutional fact or intention.

### Recommended

A recommended statement proposes an action, emphasis, research question, ceremony, sequence, or application of canonical doctrine.

It must explain the evidence and reasoning that make the recommendation relevant. It must not imply that the institution has accepted the recommendation.

### Unknown

Use Unknown when available evidence does not establish a material point. An explicit unknown is preferable to unsupported completion.

### Institution-Provided

Institution-provided material must be intentionally supplied for the work. Record:

- who provided it;
- when it was provided;
- its stated authority and status;
- confidentiality or distribution restrictions;
- whether it may be quoted, summarized, or used only for internal analysis; and
- conflicts with public information, if any.

Institution-provided information must remain distinguishable from public evidence.

## Financial information

Do not invent, interpolate, or silently normalize financial figures.

For assets, deposits, loans, capital, ROE, ROA, NPL, CASA, revenue mix, growth, and similar measures, record:

- the exact period;
- currency and unit;
- whether the figure is consolidated or institution-only;
- the disclosed definition where material;
- comparative period, if used; and
- the source and page.

A calculated value must be labeled **Calculated**, show its formula, and cite every input. Do not compare measures whose definitions or periods are materially inconsistent without explaining the limitation.

## Technology signals

Public technology references may support observations about disclosed partnerships, hiring, channels, vendors, certifications, cloud use, security programs, or APIs.

They do not, without further evidence, establish:

- production architecture;
- system ownership;
- technical maturity;
- implementation quality;
- security effectiveness;
- API availability to x-change;
- the existence or readiness of an institution-specific Execution Driver; or
- the institution's willingness to adopt a proposed model.

Technology signals must remain signals, not architecture claims.

## News and time-sensitive sources

News, product availability, management roles, branch counts, financial figures, and strategic announcements can change. Record the observation date and verify material current claims as close as practical to the editorial use date.

When a current status cannot be verified, state the date through which the evidence is known.

## Prohibited practices

Do not:

- speculate about undisclosed systems, counterparties, customers, regulatory matters, or management intentions;
- present rumor, anonymous claims, search snippets, or unattributed summaries as evidence;
- infer an operating model from a product name alone;
- treat job postings as proof that a capability is deployed;
- use stale figures as current without qualification;
- combine institution-provided and public information without attribution;
- use unlawfully obtained, leaked, or inadvertently exposed material;
- create false precision from incomplete evidence; or
- cite AI-generated summaries as primary sources.

## Contradictions and corrections

When sources conflict, record the conflict and prefer the most authoritative, specific, and recent source only when that preference is justified. Do not silently discard contrary evidence.

If a published Institution Intelligence artifact contains an evidence error, correct it visibly and record the affected source and downstream artifacts that require revalidation.
