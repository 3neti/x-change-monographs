# Strategic Gap Analysis Standard

## Purpose

Strategic Gap Analysis compares reviewed Institution Intelligence with the applicable canonical doctrine and Program Guide.

It asks:

> Where does the institution appear naturally aligned, where are material questions unresolved, and where might canonical capability become relevant?

A gap does not automatically mean institutional weakness. It may represent an unknown, a deliberate operating choice, a capability boundary, an evidence limitation, or a possible area for future discovery.

## Required inputs

- Institution Intelligence accepted for analysis;
- its source and evidence registers;
- the governing Institutional Study or sector doctrine;
- the applicable canonical Program Guide;
- relevant Editorial Decision Records; and
- approved terminology and Treasury grammar.

Do not analyze against an imagined future architecture.

## Required structure

### Executive Assessment

Summarize the principal alignments, gaps, unknowns, and implications for editorial mapping. Do not present recommendations as institutional commitments.

### Canonical Baseline

Identify the exact doctrine and Program Guide versions used. Summarize only the canonical propositions necessary to understand the comparison.

### Current Institutional Position

Provide a concise evidence-based statement of the institution's relevant current position. Cite the Institution Intelligence claims rather than repeating unsupported narrative.

### Alignment Matrix

Use at least these columns:

| Canonical area | Institutional evidence | Classification | Alignment assessment | Gap or unknown | Possible implication | Required follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Permitted assessment labels are:

- **Aligned** — evidence indicates a natural relationship to canonical doctrine.
- **Partially Aligned** — some relevant capability or priority is evidenced, but scope or operating fit remains incomplete.
- **Unknown** — available evidence does not establish the matter.
- **Potential Opportunity** — evidence supports further evaluation, not a conclusion.
- **Potential Constraint** — evidence indicates a dependency, limitation, or decision requiring attention.
- **Not Applicable** — the area does not appear relevant, with a written explanation.

### Relationship and Transaction Gravity

Assess which recurring customer, employer, merchant, government, community, or institutional relationships appear strategically important.

Ask:

- Where does the institution already participate in recurring financial activity?
- Where might usefulness across additional needs deepen relationships?
- Which observations support a Transaction Gravity analysis?
- Which conclusions would exceed the evidence?

Transaction Gravity must remain an institutional tendency, not a guaranteed commercial result.

### Banking Ceremony Relevance

Evaluate possible ceremony relevance without selecting the final editorial ceremony prematurely. Consider remittance, payroll, collections, merchant services, government flows, housing, SME workflows, or other institution-evidenced services.

For each candidate, state:

- supporting evidence;
- institutional participants;
- recurring relationship potential;
- canonical concepts exercised;
- material unknowns; and
- whether institution-provided discovery is required.

### Reusable Operating Grammar

Identify where one governed operating capability might reduce duplicated service-specific work. Do not assume that visible channel duplication proves technical duplication.

Separate:

- evidence about current services;
- inference about possible reusable grammar; and
- recommendation for later discovery.

### Institution-Specific Driver

State whether public evidence identifies relevant APIs, settlement relationships, or integration signals. Do not imply that the bank-specific driver is automatically available.

Any future driver would require institution-specific discovery, API mapping, implementation, certification, and continuing maintenance. The analysis may identify this requirement; it may not claim readiness.

### Likely Starting Questions

Identify what implementation discovery would need to establish, such as customer priority, operating ownership, settlement authority, API availability, evidence requirements, channel responsibilities, and readiness governance.

Do not turn these questions into a roadmap or proposed scope.

### Analytical Conclusions

State:

- strongest evidence-backed alignments;
- most important unknowns;
- issues that may affect editorial emphasis;
- matters requiring institution-provided discovery; and
- any potential doctrinal conflict requiring governance review.

## Traceability rule

Every analytical finding must cite both:

1. the institutional evidence on which it depends; and
2. the canonical concept against which it is assessed.

If either side is missing, classify the matter as Unknown or incomplete.

## Acceptance criteria

Strategic Gap Analysis is ready for Editorial Mapping only when:

- the canonical baseline is explicit;
- institutional evidence is traceable;
- gaps are not framed as unsupported deficiencies;
- unknowns remain visible;
- ceremony relevance is evidence-based;
- driver requirements are not overstated;
- recommendations do not become architecture; and
- possible doctrinal conflicts are escalated rather than resolved silently.
