# Institution Research Workflow

## Purpose

This workflow turns an authorized institution assignment into reviewable Institution Intelligence. It does not authorize an Institution Edition, proposal, or implementation commitment.

## Workflow

```text
Authorize institution scope
        ↓
Define research questions
        ↓
Discover and register sources
        ↓
Extract and classify evidence
        ↓
Draft Institution Intelligence
        ↓
Validate claims and limitations
        ↓
Approve intelligence for analysis
```

## 1. Authorize the scope

Record:

- institution name and approved identity;
- governing sector doctrine and Program Guide;
- intended audience and decision context;
- public-source cutoff date;
- institution-provided materials, if any;
- known restrictions; and
- explicitly excluded work.

Do not begin institution research from an ambiguous entity name or an assumed canonical lineage.

## 2. Define research questions

Translate the Institution Intelligence Standard into questions appropriate to the institution. Questions should cover identity, ownership, markets, products, finances, digital channels, technology signals, strategy, strengths, constraints, and potential alignment.

Do not let a preferred solution determine the questions.

## 3. Discover sources

Search first for primary and authoritative sources. Register each material source before relying on it in narrative.

Use the template source register to record authority, period, access date, relevant sections, and limitations. Duplicate reporting of the same announcement does not create independent confirmation.

## 4. Extract evidence

Record evidence as claims, not copied archives. Each evidence entry should contain:

- a concise claim;
- classification as Observed, Inferred, Recommended, Unknown, or Institution-Provided;
- source IDs;
- relevant date or period;
- confidence and limitations; and
- the Institution Intelligence section it supports.

Keep direct quotations short and necessary. Prefer accurate paraphrase with precise citation.

## 5. Reconcile evidence

Before drafting:

- resolve identity and reporting-period mismatches;
- distinguish institution facts from group or parent-company facts;
- identify contradictions;
- separate current products from announced or discontinued products;
- distinguish channel availability from operational capability;
- identify missing evidence; and
- record any calculation methodology.

Unresolved conflicts remain visible.

## 6. Draft Institution Intelligence

Draft from the evidence register, not from memory or search-result summaries. Write the Executive Summary last.

Use the report structure in `INSTITUTION_INTELLIGENCE_STANDARD.md`. Cite material factual claims and preserve the statement classifications in prose or supporting tables.

## 7. Validate

Perform four reviews:

### Source validation

Confirm that citations resolve, sources support the stated claims, and reporting periods are correct.

### Classification validation

Confirm that inferences and recommendations are not written as observations.

### Completeness validation

Confirm that required sections are either supported or explicitly marked Unknown. Templates must not be filled with unsupported generalities.

### Freshness validation

Recheck time-sensitive claims that materially affect institutional characterization or later editorial mapping.

## 8. Record review status

Institution Intelligence may use these statuses:

- **Not Started**
- **Researching**
- **Draft**
- **In Review**
- **Accepted for Analysis**
- **Superseded**

`Accepted for Analysis` means the artifact is sufficient for Strategic Gap Analysis. It does not mean every institutional fact is known.

## Research change control

New evidence may update Institution Intelligence. When it changes a material finding:

1. identify the changed claim;
2. cite the new or corrected source;
3. record the prior treatment;
4. assess affected analysis, mapping, editions, proposals, and roadmaps; and
5. revalidate downstream artifacts before reuse.

## Research stop conditions

Stop and report rather than proceed when:

- the institution cannot be identified reliably;
- essential claims depend on inaccessible or unverifiable material;
- public evidence materially conflicts without a defensible resolution;
- requested work would require speculation;
- confidential material lacks clear use authority; or
- the research appears to require a change to canonical doctrine rather than an institution-specific application.
