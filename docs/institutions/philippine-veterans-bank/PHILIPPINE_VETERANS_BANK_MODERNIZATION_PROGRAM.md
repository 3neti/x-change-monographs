## Executive Summary

Philippine Veterans Bank occupies a distinctive position in Philippine banking. It is a private commercial bank, a statutory veterans institution, and an official government depository. Its public record also shows active relationships with government institutions, local governments, the military community, veterans and pensioners, and selected public-finance programs. [CLM-001, CLM-004, CLM-005, CLM-011 through CLM-013, CLM-019 through CLM-023, CLM-042, CLM-043]

That position does not begin with technology. It begins with institutional purpose, lawful banking authority, established constituencies, and public responsibility.

PVB has already moved beyond a branch-only operating model. Retail and institutional digital banking, electronic payment connections, bill payment, kiosks, gateways, cards, electronic statements, and selected technology and data initiatives are publicly evidenced. [CLM-014 through CLM-018, CLM-038, CLM-040, CLM-041]

The Strategic Assessment concluded that PVB's central question is not whether modernization is relevant. It is how the bank should sequence public-finance participation, veterans and military relationships, commercial growth, capital and regulatory obligations, digital modernization, and possible inorganic expansion without weakening institutional control or overextending execution capacity. [CLM-024 through CLM-031, CLM-044]

The Gap Analysis then compared PVB's evidenced position with the canonical Government Depository capability. It found clear alignment in banking authority, government-depository status, and public-finance relevance. It found partial evidence around Treasury, digital execution, records, governance, and provider integration. It preserved as Unknown the complete operating model for authority, fund state, conditions, exceptions, reconciliation, deployment ownership, production governance, and readiness.

The proposed institutional response is the **Philippine Veterans Bank Modernization Program**.

The Program would allow PVB to evaluate one reusable governed operating foundation for selected public-finance services. The enduring result would be a maintained **Capability** through which PVB could relate authorized public instructions, recognized financial resources, service rules, participant conditions, execution, evidence, exceptions, and reconciliation without creating a separate operating foundation for each workflow.

PVB would remain the bank. Government entities would retain public authority, program purpose, participant basis, and public accountability. PVB would retain banking authority, custody, accounts, infrastructure, credentials, data, production decisions, settlement relationships, records, and institutional readiness. x-change would supply the governed operating grammar. Institution-specific integration would connect that grammar to PVB's approved execution and settlement capabilities.

The Program does not confer depository authority, government selection, procurement preference, deposits, settlement access, production readiness, or commercial return. Those outcomes remain subject to lawful process, institutional decision, operating evidence, and sustained performance.

The strongest evidence-supported first discovery candidate is a **bounded public-assistance disbursement ceremony**. PVB has disclosed a student-allowance pilot with Parañaque and a financial-assistance relationship with BARMM. [CLM-019, CLM-021] These examples provide direct institutional relevance and a bounded public-service context. Their current status, authority, account structure, scale, evidence, exception model, and readiness still require Management Validation.

Public collections form a credible secondary discovery candidate because PVB has disclosed Bills Pay PH, a city-hall kiosk, a provincial payment gateway, and named provider relationships. [CLM-017, CLM-018, CLM-020, CLM-022] Veterans' benefits and government payroll carry high strategic relevance to PVB's identity but require clearer evidence of current workflows before they are prioritized. Treasury and GOCC services remain important institutional domains; procurement settlement remains deferred until direct workflow evidence exists.

The controlled recommendation is therefore narrow: authorize Management to validate the Program as an institutional capability and define whether one bounded public-assistance ceremony is suitable for detailed design. That authorization would begin institutional discovery. It would not authorize implementation or production.

The decision before PVB is not whether to buy another channel. It is whether the institution should formally evaluate one governed operating Capability through which its banking authority and distinctive public purpose may support selected public-finance responsibilities with clearer state, evidence, reconciliation, and institutional control.

## 1. How the Program Emerged

> **Executive question:** What did the evidence establish before this Program was designed?

> **Key message:** The Program follows the evidence. It was not assumed by the research.

> **Executive decision:** I understand the evidentiary path from institution observation to institutional design.

### 1.1 What did we observe?

**Current Institution.** PVB combines three identities that must remain visible together: private commercial bank, statutory veterans institution, and official government depository. [CLM-001, CLM-004, CLM-005]

**Current Institution.** It serves retail, corporate, government, military, veteran, pensioner, and institutional markets through deposits, lending, Treasury, trust, cash management, payments, and digital services. [CLM-011 through CLM-013]

**Current Institution.** Its public record documents selected public-finance activity: an LGU student-allowance pilot, a city-hall kiosk, a provincial payment-gateway arrangement, a regional financial-assistance relationship, government lending, payroll and pension products, and military-sector engagement. [CLM-012, CLM-013, CLM-019 through CLM-023]

**Current Institution.** PVB has visible digital and payment capability and a declared transformation agenda. It also faces material capital, regulatory, asset-quality, acquisition, and management-sequencing considerations. [CLM-014 through CLM-018, CLM-024 through CLM-031, CLM-038, CLM-040, CLM-041, CLM-044]

### 1.2 What does it mean?

The Strategic Assessment found that PVB's differentiation comes from the combination of mandate, authority, constituency, relationships, and operating history. Government-depository status is significant, but it does not establish selection or readiness. Visible digital services demonstrate progress, but not the complete architecture or operating control beneath them.

The bank is already modernizing. The institutional issue is whether its public-finance services can develop through a coherent and reusable operating foundation while PVB preserves its authority, sovereignty, regulatory discipline, and distinctive purpose.

### 1.3 Compared to what?

The Gap Analysis compared the evidenced institution with the canonical Government Depository capability.

PVB is aligned with the institutional field. It possesses banking authority and government-depository status, maintains relevant constituencies, and demonstrates selected public-finance participation.

The complete canonical operating model is not evidenced as PVB's present capability. Funding Events, service-level Settlement Resources, Liens, Facilities, Settlement Envelopes, Pay Codes, governed Execution, end-to-end evidence, and reconciliation therefore entered the comparison as Proposed Program Capabilities—not as findings of deficiency.

Operating sovereignty, interfaces, exception management, deployment ownership, production governance, and readiness remain Unknown or require Management Validation.

### 1.4 The institutional response

The evidence supports neither a conclusion that PVB should remain unchanged nor a claim that it is ready for implementation. It supports a more disciplined next step: define an institution-specific Program through which PVB can decide whether a reusable governed public-finance capability is appropriate.

That is the purpose of this Program.

## 2. Why Modernization?

> **Executive question:** Why should PVB consider an institutional modernization program now?

> **Key message:** PVB already possesses authority, relationships, and digital capability. The opportunity is to govern selected public-finance services through one reusable institutional foundation.

> **Executive decision:** I understand why the evidence supports formal evaluation without presuming implementation.

PVB's modernization case begins with responsibility. Public money arrives with authority, purpose, conditions, participants, records, and an obligation to account for completion and unresolved outcomes.

PVB already provides or supports services in which those responsibilities arise. Government deposits and financing, pensions, payroll, public assistance, collections, gateways, kiosks, and institutional relationships are visible in the evidence. [CLM-012, CLM-013, CLM-019 through CLM-023]

Payment connectivity addresses only part of the work. A rail can move value. A gateway can carry an instruction. Neither, by itself, establishes the public purpose, participant basis, financial state, conditions, exception ownership, evidence, or reconciliation of the complete service.

PVB's disclosed transformation initiatives show that the institution is strengthening channels, data, Treasury, fraud management, financial-crime controls, and customer experience. [CLM-024, CLM-038, CLM-040] The proposed Program would not replace those initiatives. It would provide a common institutional grammar through which selected services may be defined and governed.

The timing must remain disciplined. Capital, leverage, regulatory applications, asset quality, acquisition activity, and management capacity affect what PVB may responsibly undertake. [CLM-025, CLM-029 through CLM-031, CLM-041, CLM-044] Modernization should therefore begin with validation and one bounded institutional question—not an expansive implementation commitment.

**Controlled Recommendation.** PVB should evaluate whether a reusable governed operating Capability can strengthen selected public-finance services while preserving banking authority, operating sovereignty, and readiness as institutional judgments.

## 3. The Institutional Opportunity

> **Executive question:** What opportunity is specific to Philippine Veterans Bank?

> **Key message:** PVB can relate its veterans purpose, government-depository position, banking authority, and public-sector relationships through one coherent public-finance capability.

> **Executive decision:** I understand why the opportunity belongs to PVB rather than to a generic institution.

PVB's opportunity is not to become a government entity or a technology provider. It is to become a stronger institutional option for selected public-finance responsibilities that fit its authority, purpose, relationships, and operating capacity.

Its veterans identity provides a continuing institutional purpose. Its commercial-bank authority provides the regulated banking foundation. Its government-depository status provides eligibility for selected government relationships. Its military, pensioner, LGU, regional-government, and institutional activities provide evidence of practical relevance. [CLM-001, CLM-004, CLM-005, CLM-011 through CLM-013, CLM-019 through CLM-023]

The opportunity is therefore broader than one transaction type. It includes the ability to examine assistance, pensions and benefits, payroll, collections, Treasury, and other approved workflows through a consistent operating discipline.

This does not imply entitlement. PVB must still earn selection through lawful authority, qualification, service design, financial standing, price, control quality, evidence, and performance.

The institutional value lies in adaptability. Resilience allows PVB to continue through difficulty. Adaptability would allow it to evaluate new public responsibilities through an established foundation rather than reconstructing the same operating disciplines for every service.

**Management Validation.** PVB must confirm how government-depository banking, veterans and military service, commercial growth, digital modernization, and any acquisition fit within the Board's current strategy and risk appetite.

## 4. Proposed Institutional Capability

> **Executive question:** What capability would the Program establish?

> **Key message:** The Program would establish one maintained operating foundation connecting public authority, banking responsibility, financial state, conditions, execution, evidence, and reconciliation.

> **Executive decision:** I understand the proposed Capability and can distinguish it from PVB's current evidenced position.

**Proposed Capability.** The Program would establish a maintained institutional ability to turn properly authorized public-finance instructions into observable, executable, evidentiary, and reconcilable banking activity.

The Program is the institutional initiative. The Capability is the enduring result.

The Capability would organize:

- the public authority and purpose supplied by the responsible government entity;
- PVB's banking service, policies, controls, accounts, and institutional responsibility;
- the recognition and reservation of operational financial resources;
- participant eligibility, conditions, approvals, and required evidence;
- the approved instruction and participant presentation;
- execution through PVB's approved settlement capabilities;
- status, exception, evidence, and reconciliation; and
- continuing institutional governance and readiness.

The Capability would not create public authority, funds, custody, settlement access, a government entitlement, or an automatic commercial outcome.

x-change would supply the maintained operating grammar. Institution-specific integration would connect that grammar to PVB's approved systems, providers, accounts, execution paths, and evidence. Any such integration would require discovery, mapping, implementation, validation, certification, and maintenance under PVB governance.

**Current Institution.** PVB has visible banking, payment, provider, Treasury, public-finance, and digital capabilities. [CLM-011 through CLM-022, CLM-040]

**Management Validation.** The current architecture, integration model, operating state, ownership boundaries, reconciliation controls, and readiness are not established by public evidence. [CLM-046, CLM-049; UNK-002 through UNK-005]

**Controlled Recommendation.** Treat the proposed Capability as an institutional evaluation object. Do not treat it as an implementation commitment until the current state, authority, first ceremony, governance, economics, architecture, and readiness requirements are validated.

## 5. Candidate Public-Finance Ceremonies

> **Executive question:** Where should PVB begin its evaluation?

> **Key message:** Begin with the most evidenced and bounded public responsibility, while preserving strategically important candidates for later validation.

> **Executive decision:** I understand the recommended discovery sequence and the evidence supporting it.

### 5.1 Primary discovery candidate — bounded public-assistance disbursement

**Current Institution.** PVB disclosed a BDA-based student-allowance pilot with the City Government of Parañaque and an agreement supporting financial-assistance disbursement to eligible BARMM beneficiaries. [CLM-019, CLM-021]

These activities provide the clearest evidence that PVB has encountered a public entity, approved participants, public funding, a disbursement purpose, a delivery journey, and a need for evidence.

**Controlled Recommendation.** Use one bounded public-assistance disbursement ceremony as the primary candidate for institutional discovery.

The recommendation is based on:

- direct PVB evidence rather than generic sector relevance;
- a public purpose that can be stated clearly;
- the possibility of a bounded participant group, value, duration, geography, and authority;
- enough operating familiarity to support meaningful management discussion; and
- the ceremony's ability to expose authority, funding, conditions, execution, evidence, exceptions, and reconciliation as one institutional responsibility.

**Management Validation.** PVB must confirm whether either disclosed relationship remains active and suitable; which public authority would participate; the source and character of funds; account and custody structure; participant basis; channels; values and volumes; provider path; evidence; exceptions; reconciliation; support; legal and regulatory conditions; and readiness.

This is a recommendation for discovery, not production selection.

### 5.2 Secondary discovery candidate — public collections

**Current Institution.** PVB has disclosed Bills Pay PH, an ECPay-supported city-hall kiosk, a Paynamics-supported provincial gateway, and public collection relationships. [CLM-017, CLM-018, CLM-020, CLM-022]

**Controlled Recommendation.** Preserve one public-collection ceremony as a secondary candidate. It may provide a complementary view because value moves toward a public obligation and requires payer attribution, obligation references, provider evidence, exception handling, and reconciliation.

**Management Validation.** Confirm current activity, public authority, obligation mapping, settlement account, provider contracts, reversals, reconciliation, service levels, support, and economics before prioritization.

### 5.3 Strategic identity candidates — veterans' benefits and government payroll

**Current Institution.** PVB's charter preserves a veterans purpose, and its disclosed products and strategy include pensioners, payroll, military personnel, and veterans. [CLM-005, CLM-011, CLM-012, CLM-023]

These ceremonies may align most directly with PVB's identity and recurring institutional relationships.

**Controlled Recommendation.** Preserve veterans' pensions or benefits and government or military payroll as strategic candidates for a later design horizon. Do not prioritize them solely on thematic fit.

**Management Validation.** Establish active program authority, current workflow, responsible institutions, participant scale, entitlement or employer data, accounts, channels, operating performance, exceptions, and strategic priority.

### 5.4 Deferred candidates — Treasury, GOCC, and procurement settlement

**Current Institution.** PVB offers Treasury and public-sector financing services and has disclosed a Treasury technology initiative. [CLM-011 through CLM-013, CLM-040]

**Controlled Recommendation.** Preserve Treasury and GOCC services for later institutional evaluation. Defer procurement settlement until PVB-specific workflow evidence exists.

Deferral is not rejection. It reflects the present evidence boundary and the need to avoid designing a ceremony from canonical examples rather than PVB facts.

## 6. Operating Model

> **Executive question:** How would the proposed Capability operate?

> **Key message:** Public authority remains public, banking authority remains with PVB, and the operating grammar coordinates the accountable ceremony between them.

> **Executive decision:** I understand where authority, funds, service meaning, execution, evidence, and reconciliation would reside.

The proposed institutional sequence is:

```text
Authorized Public Program
        ↓
PVB Depository Banking Service
        ↓
Governed Operating Capability
        ↓
PVB-Specific Execution Capability
        ↓
PVB-Approved Settlement Arrangement
```

The government entity would establish or administer public purpose, legal authority, participant basis, funding authority, approvals, program conditions, and public accountability.

PVB would define the banking service, apply policy and controls, maintain accounts and custody, govern financial state, approve execution and settlement relationships, supervise operations, retain evidence, and determine institutional readiness.

The operating grammar would keep each responsibility distinct.

A **Funding Event** would record the external financial event through which resources become recognized operationally. It would not create or characterize public money.

**Treasury** would maintain operational visibility of the Settlement Resources supporting the service. A **Lien** would reserve capacity for an authorized purpose while an instruction remains outstanding. Neither would replace a bank account or transfer custody.

A **Facility** would give the banking service its purpose, participants, policies, limits, approved commercial treatment, and institutional owner. The government program would remain the source of public authority.

A **Settlement Envelope** would carry the approvals, identity, eligibility, documents, limits, signatures, references, and other conditions required before execution.

A **Pay Code** or approved presentation would represent the authorized instruction. It would be a credential—not money, a benefit, a deposit, stored value, or the banking service.

**Execution** would coordinate the approved outcome through PVB's institution-specific execution and settlement capability. Status, provider references, exceptions, and evidence would return to the operating record for state resolution and reconciliation.

**Management Validation.** PVB must map each proposed concept against its current accounts, systems, products, policies, providers, interfaces, procedures, records, and control owners. Existing institutional controls should be reused where they are authoritative and sufficient.

## 7. Governance

> **Executive question:** Who would remain responsible?

> **Key message:** Governance follows existing public and banking responsibility. The Program does not transfer authority to software or providers.

> **Executive decision:** I understand the proposed decision owners and the limits of Program authorization.

The Board or delegated authority would establish institutional purpose, risk appetite, scope, accountability, and the authority for Management to evaluate the Program.

Management would name the sponsor and accountable leaders. Business, Operations, Treasury, Technology, Risk, Compliance, Legal, Information Security, Privacy, Finance, Accounting, Internal Audit, and other responsible functions would perform their established duties.

The responsible government entity would retain public-program authority, participant basis, funding authority, approvals, conditions, reporting, and public accountability.

ODTI would license and maintain x-change and agreed supporting capability. An approved engineering provider could perform institution-specific discovery and integration. A managed-service provider could perform delegated operational work. Settlement and payment providers would perform supported financial movement under their agreements.

None would become PVB, the government entity, custodian, public authority, auditor, or production decision-maker.

The Program would use distinct gates:

1. Program evaluation authority;
2. public and banking authority and suitability;
3. first-ceremony definition;
4. architecture, data, security, and control approval;
5. technical validation and certification;
6. operational readiness;
7. bounded production decision; and
8. evidence review and any decision to Proceed, Correct, Extend, or Pause.

**Management Validation.** Confirm PVB's actual committees, delegated authorities, procurement and outsourcing requirements, regulator engagement, control-function roles, and decision records before the proposed governance is adopted.

Board approval would begin institutional evaluation. It would not place PVB or any public program into production.

## 8. Operating Sovereignty

> **Executive question:** Would PVB retain institutional control?

> **Key message:** The proposed model preserves PVB ownership or control of its operating boundary, direct relationships, data, credentials, releases, evidence, and production decisions.

> **Executive decision:** I understand the sovereignty principle and the current evidence that must be validated.

**Proposed Capability.** PVB would own or control its approved environment, domains, deployment, databases, institutional data, encryption and access configuration, production credentials, provider accounts, monitoring, logs, backups, recovery, release approvals, records, and audit trail.

PVB would retain direct government, customer, depositor, provider, and settlement relationships. Common licensed software would not create a common bank, shared application tenancy, shared data, pooled funds, shared credentials, or shared production authority.

Delegated service would change who performs approved work. It would not transfer ownership or institutional accountability.

**Current Institution.** PVB's public record names digital services and providers but does not establish current production architecture or sovereignty boundaries. [CLM-018, CLM-046]

**Management Validation.** Confirm the present and proposed ownership of infrastructure, data, credentials, provider relationships, source and configuration, deployment, change, monitoring, backup, recovery, incident response, audit evidence, and exit rights.

Sovereignty is therefore a Program requirement, not a claim about PVB's current architecture.

## 9. Readiness

> **Executive question:** What would PVB need to know before carrying a live public responsibility?

> **Key message:** Readiness is an institutional judgment supported by complete-ceremony evidence, not a technical declaration.

> **Executive decision:** I understand why research approval, software availability, integration, and certification do not authorize production.

PVB is ready for institution-specific Program design at the editorial level because the evidence, assessment, and comparison are complete. This does not establish PVB institutional Program readiness.

The evidence corpus does not establish a PVB sponsor, authority to evaluate x-change, approved first ceremony, production architecture, interface model, service economics, operating evidence, or readiness decision. [CLM-046 through CLM-049; UNK-002 through UNK-008, UNK-010]

Readiness would advance through separate judgments:

- **Program readiness** — PVB has approved the purpose, sponsor, scope, governance, resources, and next decision for evaluation.
- **Workflow readiness** — one public-finance ceremony has clear authority, participants, funds, accounts, service ownership, conditions, evidence, exceptions, reconciliation, and decision owners.
- **Implementation readiness** — responsibilities, environments, interfaces, data, providers, security, controls, agreements, acceptance, and project governance are sufficient to begin controlled work.
- **Technical readiness** — institution-specific integration performs the approved contract under tested conditions.
- **Operational readiness** — people, policies, systems, funds, providers, accounting, support, evidence, reconciliation, security, and continuity can operate as one service.
- **Limited-production readiness** — the responsible institutions approve a narrow live scope with monitoring, support, pause criteria, and success measures.
- **Production readiness** — evidence supports continuing responsibility for the defined service.
- **Expansion readiness** — evidence supports one specifically bounded extension.

No level follows automatically from the one before it.

**Management Validation.** PVB must determine how current capital, regulatory applications, sanctions or conditions, acquisition activity, resources, risk appetite, and operating capacity affect the timing and scope of any Program decision. [CLM-025, CLM-030, CLM-031, CLM-041, CLM-044]

## 10. Controlled Implementation

> **Executive question:** What would happen if PVB decided to proceed beyond Program design?

> **Key message:** Implementation would begin with understanding one institutional responsibility and would advance only through evidence-based gates.

> **Executive decision:** I understand the controlled path without treating this Program as an implementation authorization.

This section defines the institutional philosophy of implementation. It is not a schedule, technical design, statement of work, budget, or production commitment.

### Stage 1 — Management validation

PVB would validate strategy, authority, public-finance relationships, regulatory capacity, candidate ceremonies, current architecture, operating sovereignty, interfaces, evidence, reconciliation, economics, and accountable owners.

### Stage 2 — First-ceremony definition

If a suitable public-assistance ceremony is confirmed, the responsible institutions would define public purpose, authority, funds, participants, banking service, Facility, conditions, presentation, execution, support, exceptions, evidence, reconciliation, and completion.

The participant experience would be defined as part of the banking service, not as a separate design exercise.

### Stage 3 — Institutional design and integration discovery

PVB and approved providers would map current controls and systems to the proposed Capability. They would define the institution-controlled environment, data and security boundaries, interfaces, provider behavior, operating procedures, records, acceptance, support, and maintenance.

### Stage 4 — Complete-ceremony validation

The responsible functions would test successful execution and the conditions that reveal institutional readiness: invalid authority, insufficient resources, duplicate instruction, delayed or ambiguous provider response, partial failure, invalid credential, incorrect participant data, reversal or correction, outage, compromised access, reconciliation difference, and evidence retrieval.

The result would show what people and institutions do—not only what software does.

### Stage 5 — Bounded production decision

Only after operational readiness would the responsible authorities consider limited production. Scope could be bounded by public entity, participant group, value, volume, geography, channel, operating window, Funding Events, or duration.

Success measures would be agreed before operation and could include correct authority, participant attribution, execution outcomes, reconciliation completeness, unresolved-item aging, service availability, participant evidence, support performance, security, and control effectiveness.

### Stage 6 — Evidence review

The responsible institutions would decide to **Proceed**, **Correct**, **Extend**, or **Pause**. Expansion would apply only to the approved dimension and would require a new responsibility judgment.

## 11. The Institutional Case

> **Executive question:** What would make the Program institutionally worthwhile?

> **Key message:** The Program is worthwhile only if it strengthens PVB's capacity to carry selected public-finance responsibilities without weakening control, accountability, or financial discipline.

> **Executive decision:** I understand the institutional case and the conditions that prevent it from becoming a promise of commercial return.

The case begins with PVB's identity. A veterans institution with commercial-bank authority and government-depository status has a credible reason to examine how public purpose and banking responsibility can be joined through dependable operation.

The Capability could allow PVB to evaluate multiple public-finance services through one maintained grammar. Institutional effort could focus on the aspects that genuinely differ: authority, participants, service terms, conditions, evidence, public purpose, and readiness.

Recurring services may strengthen relationships where the actual program makes PVB useful across more of an institution's or participant's needs. Veterans benefits, payroll, assistance, collections, and Treasury activity each create potential recurring contact. The evidence does not establish that they will produce deposits, retention, preferred status, or revenue. [CLM-047, CLM-049]

Commercial sustainability would require transparent accounting for licensing, institution-specific integration, infrastructure, operations, providers, settlement, security, support, reconciliation, maintenance, and continuing assurance. Potential value would remain contingent on approved service income, actual deposits, recurring activity, reduced duplicated work, stronger relationships, and public value.

**Management Validation.** No public evidence establishes the economics of a PVB-specific Program or candidate ceremony. A later commercial assessment would require current costs, provider charges, internal effort, volume, deposits, pricing authority, risk allocation, and public-service value.

The institutional case is therefore capability before return. PVB should proceed only if the proposed foundation improves its ability to govern public-finance responsibility within its strategic, financial, regulatory, and operating capacity.

## 12. Decisions Required

> **Executive question:** What decision does this Program ask PVB to consider?

> **Key message:** The immediate decision is whether to authorize disciplined institutional validation—not implementation or production.

> **Executive decision:** I understand the bounded decision and the evidence Management must return before any later commitment.

The Program presents four decisions for consideration.

### Decision 1 — Accept the institutional premise for evaluation

Determine whether PVB's combined identity, authority, relationships, and public-finance activity justify formal evaluation of one reusable governed operating Capability.

### Decision 2 — Authorize Management validation

If the premise is accepted, authorize designated PVB functions to validate the material Unknowns in the evidence, assessment, and Gap Analysis.

### Decision 3 — Validate the first discovery candidate

Determine whether one bounded public-assistance disbursement ceremony is active, authorized, strategically relevant, operationally familiar, sufficiently bounded, and suitable for detailed design. If it is not, return to the evidence-supported candidate set rather than forcing selection.

### Decision 4 — Require a separate return decision

Require Management to return with:

- confirmed institutional purpose and sponsor;
- validated public and banking authority;
- current financial, regulatory, and resource conditions;
- a defined first ceremony and responsible public counterpart;
- current-state and proposed operating boundaries;
- governance and sovereignty model;
- preliminary economic assessment;
- implementation-readiness dependencies;
- unresolved Unknowns and risks; and
- the exact next decision requested.

No decision in this Program authorizes procurement, contracting, system access, integration, production, a public program, or settlement activity.

The first decision authorizes institutional learning. Every later decision must be supported by the evidence required for the responsibility PVB is being asked to carry.

### Appendix E — Canonical Capability Boundaries

- Public authority remains with the government entity.
- Banking authority, custody, accounts, and settlement relationships remain with PVB.
- Treasury represents operational state; it does not replace accounts or determine legal fund character.
- A Funding Event recognizes an external event; it does not create funds or authority.
- A Lien reserves operational capacity; it does not transfer custody.
- A Facility defines the banking service; it does not replace the public program.
- A Settlement Envelope carries conditions and evidence; it does not settle funds.
- A Pay Code is a credential; it is not money, a benefit, or the service.
- Execution coordinates the approved outcome through PVB's approved capability.
- Technical certification does not establish operational readiness.
- Common software does not create a common bank.

Canonical definitions remain governed by the [Government Depository Program Guide](../../editorial/program-guides/government-depository/government-depository-digital-banking-program-guide.md) and its appendices.
