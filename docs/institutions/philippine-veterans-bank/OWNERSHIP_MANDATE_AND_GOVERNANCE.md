# Philippine Veterans Bank — Ownership, Mandate, and Governance

## Scope

This note separates legal authority, ownership, institutional purpose, and governance. It is not a legal opinion.

## Facts

### Revised Charter

- **Observed.** RA 11597 continues PVB as a private commercial bank. [CLM-001]
- **Observed.** The law authorizes ₱10 billion of capital stock and allows non-veterans to invest, provided veterans retain a majority of common shares at all times. [CLM-002]
- **Observed.** The term veterans includes the statutory World War II constituency and, under the law's conditions, post-World War II veterans and AFP retirees. [CLM-002]
- **Observed.** Section 28 declares PVB an official government depository and authorizes identified custodians to maintain government or corporate funds with it if they so desire. [CLM-004]
- **Observed.** The charter preserves a statutory benefit mechanism for veterans. [CLM-005]

### Government-depository framework

- **Observed.** DOF Circular No. 003-2022 included PVB in the authorized government-depository framework by amending Department Circular No. 002-2022. [SRC-002]
- **Observed.** DOF Circular No. 004-2024 further amended the government-depository list. PVB's audited report states that the amendment allows it to receive government deposits or funds without prior approval from the Secretary of Finance, subject to the applicable framework. [SRC-003, SRC-004]
- **Observed.** Neither the charter nor the circulars require a public entity to select PVB for a particular account or service. [SRC-001–SRC-003]

### Ownership

- **Observed.** RYM Business Management Corporation held 40.1392% of common shares as of 31 March 2026 and was the only disclosed holder above 5%. [CLM-008]
- **Observed.** The audited report stated that PVB had 7,567 stockholders at 31 December 2025. [SRC-004]
- **Observed.** The public record distinguishes issued shares, deposits for future stock subscriptions, qualifying capital, audited equity, appraisal increment, and authorized capital. These measures are not interchangeable. [SRC-004, SRC-006]

### Board and executive governance

- **Observed.** The charter provides for 15 directors, five of whom are independent. [CLM-003]
- **Observed.** The April 2026 disclosure identified Emmanuel Herbosa as Chairman and Eulogio Catabran III as President. [CLM-009]
- **Observed.** Board committees cover executive, audit, credit, IT, risk, governance and compliance, related parties, human resources, remuneration, trust, and asset-disposal responsibilities. [SRC-006]
- **Observed.** The disclosed Board includes banking, finance, law, military, public-service, technology, and governance backgrounds. [SRC-006]

## Observed implications

- **Inferred.** PVB's government-depository role is statutory but discretionary in application. Public institutions retain their own authority and must still determine whether a specific account, deposit, loan, collection, or disbursement arrangement is lawful and appropriate. [CLM-004, CLM-042]
- **Inferred.** Veteran-majority ownership is a continuing governance constraint as well as a defining institutional feature. Capital raising must remain consistent with both regulatory requirements and the charter. [CLM-002, CLM-031]
- **Inferred.** The Board's committee structure provides formal oversight surfaces relevant to digital, public-finance, capital, and acquisition decisions. Public records do not establish how those committees have assessed a particular future Program. [CLM-003, CLM-048]

## Outstanding questions

2. What capital actions and regulatory approvals remain outstanding after the April 2026 disclosure?
3. What Board committees would sponsor and approve a government-depository modernization assessment?
4. What account-level legal review is required for each class of government fund?
5. How are public-sector conflicts, related parties, procurement, and third-party technology arrangements governed?
6. What authority would be required before PVB personnel could provide institution-confidential evidence for a later phase?
