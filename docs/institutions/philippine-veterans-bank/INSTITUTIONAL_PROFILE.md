# Philippine Veterans Bank — Institutional Profile

## Scope

This note records institutional identity, history, footprint, constituencies, and business model. Claim provenance remains in the [Evidence Register](EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md).

## Facts

### Formation and development

- **Observed.** RA 3518 created PVB in 1963 around a veterans-benefit purpose and a banking institution intended to preserve value for World War II veterans and their families. [CLM-006]
- **Observed.** PVB closed in 1983, was rehabilitated under RA 7169, received authority to operate again as a private commercial bank in May 1992, and resumed commercial operations later that year. [CLM-006]
- **Observed.** RA 11597 became effective in 2022 and replaced the earlier charter framework. [CLM-001–CLM-002]

### Present institutional identity

- **Observed.** PVB is a private commercial bank. [CLM-001]
- **Observed.** PVB is separately declared an official government depository. [CLM-004]
- **Observed.** The charter preserves a veterans-benefit purpose and extends the relevant constituency to post-World War II veterans and AFP retirees under the stated conditions. [CLM-002, CLM-005]
- **Observed.** PVB appeared in PDIC's directory of insured universal and commercial banks as of 2 January 2024. [CLM-035]

### Footprint

- **Observed.** The strongest dated disclosure reports 60 full branches and one branch-lite at 31 December 2025. [CLM-010]
- **Observed.** The information statement reports 281 ATMs—68 onsite and 213 offsite—at the same date. [CLM-010]
- **Unknown.** Some current website surfaces show lower branch and ATM counts, and the public record does not fully reconcile ownership, operation, and network-access categories. [CLM-050]

### Markets and constituencies

- **Observed.** PVB serves individual, corporate, cooperative, and public-sector clients and identifies veterans, pensioners, active and retired uniformed personnel, and their families within its institutional constituency. [CLM-011]
- **Observed.** Public-sector markets include LGUs, NGAs, GOCCs, water districts, electric cooperatives, and other institutions. [CLM-013]
- **Observed.** The bank's disclosed offerings span deposits, lending, Treasury, trust, foreign exchange, cash management, cards, digital channels, collections, payroll, pensions, and public assistance. [CLM-011–CLM-018]

### Group structure

- **Observed.** PVB reported Intervest Projects, Inc., New Rural Bank of Agoncillo, Inc., and Veterans Venture Capital Corporation as subsidiaries at 31 December 2025. [CLM-007]
- **Observed.** New Rural Bank of Agoncillo is a separate regulated entity. Its products, assets, customers, and operations are not attributed to PVB unless a source expressly does so.

## Observed implications

- **Inferred.** PVB's identity is institutionally hybrid but legally coherent: private commercial-bank ownership and governance coexist with a veterans purpose and official government-depository authority. [CLM-042]
- **Inferred.** Its commercial model is broader than veterans banking. The public record shows a general banking franchise shaped by a specific constituency and public-finance position. [CLM-011, CLM-043]
- **Inferred.** Physical distribution remains relevant to PVB's public, pension, payroll, military, and assisted-account activities even as the bank expands digital channels. [CLM-010, CLM-014, CLM-019–CLM-021]
