## Executive Summary

**Observed.** Philippine Veterans Bank is a private commercial bank with a statutory identity rooted in Filipino veterans and a separate legal status as an official government depository. Republic Act No. 11597 widened the veteran constituency, permitted non-veteran investment while requiring veterans to retain a majority of common shares, authorized ₱10 billion of capital stock, and continued the bank's private commercial-bank status. PVB is therefore neither a government-owned bank nor an ordinary private commercial bank without a public mandate. Its private ownership, veterans purpose, and government-depository authority coexist. [CLM-001–CLM-005]

**Observed.** Public evidence shows a broad operating institution rather than a single-constituency bank. PVB reports 60 full branches and one branch-lite, a nationwide ATM footprint, retail and institutional digital banking, deposits and loans for individuals and organizations, Treasury and trust services, cash management, public-sector financing, payroll and pension services, bills payment, POS, kiosks, payment gateways, and basic-account disbursement. Its disclosed constituencies include veterans and their families, active and retired uniformed personnel, pensioners, private individuals and corporations, LGUs, NGAs, GOCCs, cooperatives, and other institutions. [CLM-010–CLM-018]

**Observed.** The bank has moved beyond legal eligibility into selected public-finance activity. Its 2025 annual report identifies electronic student-allowance disbursement for Parañaque, digital payment facilities for properties of the Province of Negros Occidental, and institutional payment capabilities involving Bills Pay PH, ECPay, and Paynamics. A 2026 announcement records an agreement with BARMM for financial-assistance disbursement and an offsite ATM. These are bounded examples. They do not establish the scale, economics, account structures, or performance of PVB's total public-finance activity. [CLM-019–CLM-022, CLM-043]

**Observed.** Management's current direction combines capital restoration, deeper military-sector relationships, customer-centric and digital transformation, public-sector participation, rebranding, and potential inorganic expansion through the proposed acquisition of UCPB Savings Bank. Retail digital banking launched in February 2025 and institutional digital banking in June 2025. The bank also disclosed payment, fraud, AML, Treasury, data-governance, card-issuance, and secure-file-transfer initiatives. [CLM-016, CLM-023–CLM-025, CLM-038–CLM-041]

**Observed.** PVB reported materially improved 2025 consolidated results: ₱96.282 billion in assets, ₱78.969 billion in deposits, ₱30.825 billion in net loans, ₱10.843 billion in equity, and ₱1.322 billion in net income. BSP's separate bank-only statistics as of 31 March 2026 reported ₱111.535 billion in assets, ₱96.600 billion in deposits, and ₱33.679 billion in net loans and receivables. These figures should not be merged without reconciling period, perimeter, and definition. [CLM-026–CLM-034]

**Observed.** The financial improvement sits beside material prudential and audit qualifications. The 2025 auditor issued a qualified opinion, identified a material uncertainty related to going concern, and described the bank's dependence on capital actions and regulatory approvals. The disclosed leverage ratio remained below the stated minimum. Reported CAR included an appraisal increment; without it, the bank would not have met the stated minimum capitalization and CAR requirements at 31 December 2025. Certain other regulatory requirements were not met, and sanctions were disclosed. Liquidity measures were nevertheless reported above their stated thresholds. [CLM-030–CLM-031]

**Inferred.** PVB's institutional significance lies in the combination of authority, constituency, and developing capability. Its charter provides a distinct public-finance position. Its military and veterans relationships provide a specific social and institutional base. Its public-sector products and recent digital initiatives show practical participation beyond symbolism. The same evidence also indicates that institutional capacity, regulatory conditions, technology maturity, and strategic execution must be assessed together rather than assumed from the charter or a product list. [CLM-042–CLM-045]

**Unknown.** Public evidence does not establish PVB's production architecture, public-fund account and reconciliation structures, API readiness, provider contracts, digital operating performance, government-deposit concentration, current acquisition outcome, or interest in x-change. These gaps require authorized institution discovery. Phase 1 supports review with stated Unknowns; it does not support a proposal or readiness declaration. [CLM-046–CLM-050]

## Method and Source Base

The dossier follows the canonical Institution Intelligence Framework and the Government Depository Institution Intelligence Research Gate. Research focused on law, DOF records, audited annual reports, the April 2026 information statement, BSP statistics, PDIC records, PVB product and service documents, and attributable partnership announcements.

Statements are classified as **Observed**, **Inferred**, **Recommended**, **Unknown**, **Institution-Provided**, or **Calculated**. No institution-provided material was used. No calculated financial measure is carried into the conclusions because audited consolidated data, bank-only BSP statistics, and published statements of condition use different periods or perimeters.

The [Source Register](SOURCE_REGISTER.md) records provenance, period, pages, claims, and limitations. The [Evidence Register](EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md) is the claim-level source of truth. Supporting domain notes summarize the same evidence and do not change its classification.

Material source conflicts remain visible. The dated SEC disclosure and audited report support 61 total branch locations—60 full branches and one branch-lite—and 281 ATMs at 31 December 2025, while some current website text shows lower totals. Press-release language that the bank achieved a ₱10 billion capitalization target is not treated as equivalent to audited equity, qualifying capital, or final regulatory approval.

This is not a legal opinion, credit opinion, prudential assessment, technology due diligence report, or endorsement of a later engagement.

## Institutional Profile

### History

**Observed.** PVB was created in 1963 from a statutory design intended to preserve value for Filipino World War II veterans and their families. It closed in 1983, was rehabilitated under RA 7169, received authority to operate again as a private commercial bank in 1992, and resumed commercial operations that year. RA 11597 replaced the earlier charter framework in 2022. [CLM-006]

### Legal identity and mandate

**Observed.** RA 11597 continues PVB as a private commercial bank. Section 28 separately declares it an official government depository and authorizes specified public custodians, if they so desire, to maintain government or corporate funds with the bank. This is an authority and eligibility proposition. It is not a mandatory allocation of government deposits or a guarantee of selection. [CLM-001, CLM-004]

**Observed.** The revised charter retains a statutory veterans-benefit purpose. It expands the relevant veteran definition to include post-World War II veterans and AFP retirees under the stated conditions and preserves veteran-majority common-share ownership. [CLM-002, CLM-005]

### Ownership and group relationships

**Observed.** RYM Business Management Corporation held 40.1392% of common shares as of 31 March 2026 and was the only disclosed holder above 5%. Public evidence does not establish a controlling shareholder above 50%. The charter nevertheless requires veterans to retain a majority of common shares collectively. [CLM-002, CLM-008]

**Observed.** PVB reported Intervest Projects, Inc., New Rural Bank of Agoncillo, Inc., and Veterans Venture Capital Corporation as subsidiaries at 31 December 2025. Their activities and legal identities are not treated as activities of the parent bank unless the disclosure expressly says so. [CLM-007]

### Governance and leadership

**Observed.** The charter provides for 15 directors, including five independent directors. The April 2026 disclosure identified Emmanuel Herbosa as Chairman and Eulogio Catabran III as President. It also described committees spanning audit, risk, IT, credit, governance and compliance, related parties, remuneration, trust, and other responsibilities. [CLM-003, CLM-009]

### Markets and constituencies served

**Observed.** PVB's public products and management narrative address veterans, AFP retirees and active personnel, pensioners, individual depositors and borrowers, employers, private corporations, cooperatives, LGUs, NGAs, GOCCs, water districts, electric cooperatives, and other institutions. [CLM-011–CLM-013, CLM-023]

**Inferred.** This creates a hybrid commercial model: broad banking activities are organized around a distinct veterans and military identity, while government-depository authority and public-sector products create a second institutional axis. The relative contribution of each market is not publicly quantified. [CLM-011, CLM-042, CLM-047]

### Branches and geographic presence

**Observed.** The strongest dated disclosure reports 61 branches and 281 ATMs as of 31 December 2025. The annual report describes 60 full branches and one branch-lite. The information statement reports 68 onsite and 213 offsite ATMs. [CLM-010]

**Unknown.** Current website counts differ, and public evidence does not fully reconcile owned, managed, onsite, offsite, and network access categories. [CLM-050]

### Business model

**Observed.** Interest income from deposits, lending, and investments is the principal revenue base, with service charges, fees, commissions, trading, foreign exchange, trust, asset sales, and other income contributing non-interest revenue. The information statement reported 73.39% of 2025 revenue from net interest income and 26.61% from other income. [SRC-006]

**Observed.** The bank combines deposit gathering, lending, investment and Treasury activity, trust and fiduciary services, cash management, card and channel services, and public-sector relationships. [CLM-011]

### Audit and prudential context

**Observed.** The 2025 independent auditor's report was qualified for the disclosed treatment of an old thrift-bank exposure and land revaluation matters. It separately identified a material uncertainty related to going concern. [SRC-004]

**Observed.** The report states that CAR included an appraisal increment and that without it PVB would not have complied with the stated minimum capitalization and CAR requirements. It also states that the CBUP depended on capital actions, investor support, and timely BSP approvals; some other regulatory requirements were not met and sanctions were imposed. [CLM-031]

**Observed.** These qualifications coexist with reported profitability, deposit growth, high LCR and NSFR, capital inflows, and management's continuing transformation activity. [CLM-026–CLM-031]

**Inferred.** The proper institutional reading is neither that the bank's progress is illusory nor that the improvement resolves all constraints. PVB entered 2026 with stronger disclosed scale and earnings while still carrying material capital, regulatory, and execution dependencies. [CLM-044]

## Digital Banking Profile

### Current channels

**Observed.** PVB offers branch, ATM, online, mobile, POS, kiosk, payment-gateway, bills-payment, and card channels. Digital Banking covers individual and institutional clients and supports account viewing, bills, internal and external transfers, and e-wallet cash-in. [CLM-014, CLM-017]

### Customer journeys

**Observed.** Public retail terms show both self-registration and branch-assisted enrollment, identity validation, separate credentials, limits, alerts, account statements and history, bills payment, transfers, stop-payment requests, and security notices. [CLM-015]

**Observed.** The BDA is described as a low-cost account that institutional clients may use for beneficiary disbursement. The Parañaque example connects account opening, allowance crediting, card/ATM access, and a public program, but public evidence does not describe the complete exception and reconciliation journey. [CLM-019, CLM-049]

### Digital initiatives

**Observed.** PVB reported 32% growth in gross revenue from digital products and channels, 40% growth in total digital-channel transactions, and 13% growth in digital transactions by PVB clients in 2025. Absolute values and comparable definitions were not disclosed in the cited section. [SRC-004]

**Observed.** The bank disclosed retail and institutional platform launches, InstaPay Sender, Bills Pay PH receiving, instant card issuance pilots, eSOAs, secure file transfer, fraud and AML systems, a Treasury platform, and an Enterprise Data Office. [CLM-016–CLM-018, CLM-038–CLM-041]

## Strategic Direction

### Explicit management direction

**Observed.** PVB's published 2025 direction includes workforce, customer-centric, and digital transformation; a stronger military-sector position; deeper public-sector participation; digital products and inclusion; rebranding; and acquisition-led expansion. [CLM-023–CLM-025]

**Observed.** Management linked the AFP relationship to salary-deducted consumer lending and broader uniformed-service markets. It linked BARMM presence to cash management, consumer, digital, and SME activity and described Islamic banking concepts as exploratory. It linked the proposed UCPB Savings acquisition to public-school teacher lending and agricultural markets. [SRC-006]

### Bounded interpretation

**Inferred.** The strategy seeks to convert a distinctive charter and constituency into broader commercial relevance. Digital capability is being positioned as an operating enabler for military, government, retail, and institutional relationships rather than as a stand-alone digital-bank identity. [CLM-023–CLM-024, CLM-044]

**Inferred.** Capital, regulation, acquisition, public-sector execution, and technology modernization are interdependent workstreams. Their disclosed coexistence does not reveal Board-approved sequencing, resource allocation, or risk appetite. [CLM-025, CLM-031, CLM-044]

## Institutional Strengths

These are evidence-based attributes, not endorsements.

- **Observed — distinctive legal position.** PVB is both a private commercial bank and an official government depository. [CLM-001, CLM-004]
- **Observed — defined constituency.** The charter and product base connect the bank with veterans, AFP retirees, uniformed personnel, pensioners, and related families and institutions. [CLM-002, CLM-005, CLM-011]
- **Observed — nationwide physical distribution.** PVB disclosed 61 branch locations and a substantial onsite/offsite ATM footprint at 31 December 2025. [CLM-010]
- **Observed — broad banking license utilization.** Public products span deposits, lending, Treasury, trust, cash management, cards, digital channels, collections, and government services. [CLM-011–CLM-018]
- **Observed — practical public-sector examples.** Disclosures identify LGU and BARMM disbursement, payment-gateway, kiosk, public-sector financing, and military relationships. [CLM-019–CLM-023]
- **Observed — renewed earnings and deposit scale.** 2025 and March 2026 figures show a larger institution than the prior-year audited base. [CLM-026–CLM-034]
- **Observed — explicit transformation program.** PVB has disclosed named digital, fraud, AML, Treasury, data, and channel initiatives. [CLM-024, CLM-040–CLM-041]

## Institutional Constraints

- **Observed — capital and prudential conditions.** The audited report describes conditional capital recognition, a below-minimum leverage ratio, pending CBUP-related approvals, other non-compliance, and sanctions. [CLM-030–CLM-031]
- **Observed — audit qualifications.** The 2025 opinion is qualified and includes a material uncertainty related to going concern. [SRC-004]
- **Observed — asset-quality burden.** Consolidated NPLs remained ₱5.840 billion at 31 December 2025 despite the year-on-year reduction. [CLM-029]
- **Observed — multiple simultaneous transformations.** Capital restoration, military expansion, digital programs, rebranding, and a proposed acquisition all demand governance and execution capacity. [CLM-023–CLM-025, CLM-044]
- **Unknown — operating maturity.** Public evidence does not show absolute channel performance, public-program reconciliation, end-to-end exception handling, architecture, or service resilience. [CLM-046, CLM-049]
- **Unknown — public-finance concentration and economics.** Aggregate deposits and product lists do not show government balances, revenues, client concentration, or workflow economics. [CLM-047]

## Opportunities for Canonical Alignment

No recommendation is authorized in Phase 1. This section is therefore intentionally limited to evidence domains that a later accepted Strategic Assessment may examine:

- the relationship between PVB's government-depository authority and its evidenced public-finance services;
- the institutional controls beneath BDA disbursement, bills payment, payment gateways, kiosks, payroll, pensions, and public-sector lending;
- the boundary between bank authority, government authority, providers, accounts, and operational evidence;
- the reuse potential of PVB's disclosed digital and payment capabilities across more than one public workflow;
- the effect of capital, regulatory, acquisition, and operating constraints on sequencing; and
- whether institution discovery establishes any actual relevance for x-change.

These are research leads, not findings that PVB should adopt the Program.

## Unknowns and Research Limits

The most material Unknowns are:

1. government-deposit composition, concentration, and account structure;
2. end-to-end governance and reconciliation for public collections and disbursements;
3. current core, channel, data, integration, security, cloud, and recovery architecture;
4. API, file, provider, settlement, exception, and reversal behavior;
5. absolute digital adoption, performance, fraud, dispute, and service measures;
6. current regulatory approval and capital-plan status;
7. current UCPB Savings acquisition status and integration consequences;
8. current scope and performance of named public-sector partnerships;
9. reconciled branch and ATM ownership/operation counts; and
10. institutional interest in or authorization for any x-change assessment.

The complete question set is in [Questions for Management](QUESTIONS_FOR_MANAGEMENT.md).

## Phase 1 conclusion

**Inferred.** PVB is an institution with a distinctive legal mandate, an identifiable military and veterans constituency, a broad commercial-banking base, selected evidence of digital public-finance execution, and an active transformation agenda. It is also an institution whose audit, capital, regulatory, acquisition, and operating conditions require careful, evidence-led assessment.

The public record is sufficient to describe the institution and proceed to review with stated Unknowns. It is not sufficient to declare Program alignment, operational readiness, commercial fit, or implementation feasibility.
