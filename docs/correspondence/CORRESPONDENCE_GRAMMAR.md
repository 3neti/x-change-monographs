# Correspondence Grammar

## Correspondence

A governed record of a formal institutional communication.

## Sent Correspondence

A communication known to have been dispatched. Sent status does not, by itself, verify that the preserved body is an exact transcript.

## Draft Correspondence

A communication prepared but not yet dispatched. A draft must not be represented as historical evidence of delivery.

## Exact Sent Record

A verbatim or source-verified copy of the communication that was transmitted.

## Reconstructed Sent Record

A clearly labeled best-effort reconstruction used when the transmitted text is unavailable. It preserves known facts without claiming verbatim fidelity.

## Correspondence Template

A reusable communication pattern containing placeholders rather than historical institutional facts.

## Delivery Context

Factual information describing how the correspondence and related artifacts were transmitted, including the channel and the known state of parallel physical delivery.

## Related Submission

The governed Submission Portfolio referenced by or attached to the correspondence. The related submission retains its own canonical manifest, version, release tag, and publication boundaries.

## Verification Status

The declared state of evidence supporting the preserved correspondence text. Typical values include `verified`, `pending_exact_sent_copy`, and `not_applicable` for templates.

## Canonical distinctions

1. `status: sent` establishes dispatch, not textual fidelity.
2. `record_type: exact_sent_correspondence` requires a verbatim or source-verified body.
3. `record_type: reconstructed_sent_correspondence` requires an explicit pending-verification notice.
4. A template contains no claim that a message was delivered.
5. A Delivery Record records transmission context without becoming a copy of the related submission.
